# Brief 2A: lesson 0.2, "What is the machine made of?": question, motivation, prediction, the ladder

Read `00-module.md` (this folder) and `docs/style.md` first. Write these keys, in this order.

## Facts for this lesson

- The machine of the last lesson is paused before line 3, "R3 becomes R1 minus R2", with room A
  at -184 and room B at -250. R1 is -184 and R2 is -250.
- While it waits before line 3, the part that adds has already worked out R1 minus R2: 66. The
  line, when it runs, puts that 66 into R3.
- The part that adds is made of 64 slices, side by side. Each slice works on one place: the slice
  worth 1, the slice worth 2, the slice worth 4, and so on, each place worth twice the one before.
  Each slice gives a 1 or a 0. Together their 1s and 0s are the number, as you write a number in
  1s and 0s.
- 66 is 64 + 2, which in 1s and 0s is 1000010. So the slices worth 64 and 2 give 1; every other
  slice gives 0. Written in eight groups of eight, the 64 slices give
  `00000000 00000000 00000000 00000000 00000000 00000000 00000000 01000010`.
- Each slice is made of smaller parts, and those of the smallest parts the machine has. At the
  bottom is one wire. A wire is high or low: a high voltage stands for 1, a low voltage for 0.
- The course builds the machine from the bottom up: Module 1 starts at one wire. This lesson goes
  the other way, from the top down, to show what the course will build.
- The ladder figure has 7 levels. Each level is the real machine, opened one level further, drawn
  by the simulator with its values, with the number at that level, and the module of the course
  that builds it:
  1. The line (Module 11, "Programming and debugging"): line 3, and the number it gives, 66.
  2. The machine's parts (Module 8, "Putting pieces together"): the whole machine's drawing. It is
     wide; on a small screen a strip above it shows all of it, and "Make smaller" zooms out. The
     part that adds is marked. The number here: 66.
  3. The part that adds, opened (Module 7, "Arithmetic and logic"): four boxes, each 16 slices.
     Its 1s and 0s: the 64 above.
  4. Four slices (Module 7): the first box opened, then the first box inside it: the four lowest
     slices, worth 8, 4, 2 and 1. Their 1s and 0s: `0010`.
  5. One slice (Module 7): the slice worth 2, opened. It has a part that adds, and parts that
     choose which result goes out. It gives 1.
  6. The smallest parts (Module 3, "Selecting, comparing, adding"): inside the slice's adding part,
     the two smallest parts that make its sum. Module 2 makes these smallest parts. Its result: 1.
  7. One wire (Module 1, "Voltage to numbers"): back in the slice, the wire from its adding part,
     named SUM in the drawing. It is high: it stands for 1.
- The figure's buttons are "Up a level" and "Down a level". Each level shows "Built in Module 8:
  Putting pieces together" and so on.

## question (two short paragraphs, under 100 wds)

- In the last lesson the display showed 66, how much warmer room A is than room B. Line 3 worked
  it out: R3 becomes R1 minus R2.
- The machine was a box that runs lines. Ask: what is inside the box, and where in it does 66
  come from? End with the lesson's question.

## motivation (two short paragraphs, under 110 wds)

- The machine is made of parts. Each part is made of smaller parts, down to wires that are high or
  low.
- Each part is used as a box, its insides hidden. You can look at one level at a time.
- The course builds it from the bottom: Module 1 starts at one wire. This lesson goes down from
  the top, to show where each module's part sits in the finished machine.

## prediction (one paragraph, under 110 wds)

- The machine is paused before line 3. The part that adds has already worked out 66.
- The part that adds is 64 slices side by side. Each works on one place and gives a 1 or a 0. The
  slices are worth 1, 2, 4, 8 and so on.
- The figure opens the part that adds. Its values stay hidden until you choose an answer and press
  "Check my prediction".

## p1Question (one sentence)

- When the part that adds gives 66, which of its slices give a 1?

## p1Explain (one paragraph, under 70 wds; shown after the learner commits)

- 66 is 64 + 2. So the slice worth 64 and the slice worth 2 give 1, and the other 62 give 0. No
  slice is worth 66: each slice gives one 1 or 0, and the number is the pattern of all 64.
- Press "Down a level" to see the four lowest slices give `0010`.

## The seven level captions, one to three sentences each (under 45 wds each)

Keys: levelLine, levelParts, levelAdder, levelFour, levelSlice, levelSmallest, levelWire. Each
says what this level shows and what to look at. Use the facts above for each level. Do not repeat
the module's name (the figure shows it). In levelParts, say the drawing is wide and how to see all
of it. In levelSmallest, say Module 2 builds these smallest parts. In levelWire, say a high
voltage stands for 1 and a low one for 0.

## ladderLead (two short paragraphs, under 100 wds; a lead above the figure)

- The figure starts at line 3 and goes down a level at each press of "Down a level", to one wire.
- At each level, find the number. It is the same number all the way down: 66, then its 1s and 0s,
  then the 1 of one slice, then one wire.

## ladderAfter (one paragraph, under 70 wds; shown once the learner reaches the bottom level)

- The 66 at the top is the 1s and 0s on 64 slices' outputs. One of those, the slice worth 2, is
  one wire, high.
- Every level is the same machine, seen from nearer. The course builds it from that wire upwards.
