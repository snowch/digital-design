# Brief 2B: lesson 0.2: construction, failure experiment, explanation

Read `00-module.md` (this folder), the facts in `2A.md`, and `docs/style.md` first. Write these
keys, in this order.

## Facts

- The construction: room A reads -180 (-18.0 degrees) and room B -250. Paused before line 3, the
  part that adds works out -180 minus -250 = 70. 70 is 64 + 4 + 2, so the eight lowest slices,
  highest worth first (128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1), give `0100 0110`. The test reads the eight
  slices' outputs in the simulator. The answer is typed as eight 1s and 0s; a space between the
  two groups of four is allowed.
- The failure experiment: inside the slice worth 2, the wire from its adding part (SUM in the
  drawing, the wire at the bottom of the ladder) is stuck low, at 0, whatever drives it. Room A
  reads -184 and room B -250, as before.
- With that wire stuck, the machine runs lines 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 9, as before. The display
  shows 64, not 66. CLASH stays dark, as before. The machine stops normally and reports nothing
  wrong.
- 66 is 64 + 2; with the slice worth 2 giving 0, the part that adds gives 64.
- The figure: the machine at work, with a choice "Nothing stuck" or the stuck wire, and under it
  the drawing of the slice worth 2 with the stuck wire. Each choice starts the machine again.

## c1Hints.1 to c1Hints.5 (in this order)

1. The concept: the slices give line 3's number, R1 minus R2, in 1s and 0s.
2. The mistake: working with the readings, not the gap. The slices give -180 minus -250, not
   either reading.
3. A smaller example: for 66, which is 64 + 2, the eight slices give `0100 0010`.
4. Part of the answer: line 3 gives 70.
5. The answer: 70 is 64 + 4 + 2, so `0100 0110`.

## stuckAfter (two short paragraphs, under 100 wds; shown once the stuck machine has run)

- The display shows 64, not 66. The slice worth 2 gives 0, so the part that adds gives 64 + 0.
- Every line ran as before, the program stopped normally, and nothing on the page says anything is
  wrong. 6.4 degrees looks like a reasonable gap. One wire, four levels down, changed what the shop
  sees.

## explanation (three short paragraphs, under 150 wds)

- Each level is the level below seen from further away. The 66 in R3 is the 1s and 0s on 64 slices'
  outputs, and each of those is a wire, high or low.
- So a fault at the bottom shows at the top as a wrong number, and nothing at the top can tell. The
  display had no way to know that the 2 was missing.
- This is why the course tests every part it builds before it uses that part as a box. A part used
  as a box must do what its outside promises.
