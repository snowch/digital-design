# Brief 1A: lesson 0.1, "What does a computer do?": question, motivation, prediction, investigation

Read `00-module.md` (this folder) and `docs/style.md` first. Write these keys, in this order.

## question (two short paragraphs, under 90 wds)

- The shop has two freezer rooms, A and B, each with a sensor; an office with a display and three
  lamps, ALARM, NIGHT and CLASH.
- A machine sits between the sensors and the office. It reads the rooms and decides what the
  display shows and which lamps light.
- The figure below draws this: the two rooms' sensors on the left, the machine in the middle, the
  display and the lamps on the right.
- End with the lesson's question, in one sentence: how does the machine decide what the display
  shows?

## shopAfter (one paragraph, under 60 wds; shown under the drawing)

- Each sensor sends its room's temperature as a number, in tenths of a degree: room A at -18.4
  degrees sends -184; room B, kept colder at -25.0 degrees, sends -250.
- The 64 written beside a sensor's and the display's connection is how many wires take the
  number between them. (No forward reference: do not say where the course explains it.)

## motivation (three short paragraphs, under 150 wds)

- The machine does not decide anything by itself. It follows a program: a list of lines, numbered
  from 1, each saying one small thing to do.
- It runs one line at a time, in order, unless a line tells it to go to another line.
- It keeps sixteen numbers, named R0 to R15. A line can set one of them, read a room, show a
  number on the display, or set the lamps.
- The shop's program has 9 lines. Give them as a numbered Markdown list, exactly as the fact
  sheet gives them.
- Close: running these lines is all the machine does; the next figure asks you which of them it
  runs.

## prediction (one paragraph, under 70 wds)

- The figure shows the program and the rooms at -184 and -250. Its numbers and buttons stay hidden
  until you choose an answer.
- Work out what line 3 gives and what line 6 then does. Choose an answer and press "Check my
  prediction".

## p1Question (one sentence)

- Room A reads -184 and room B -250. Which lines does the machine run, from line 1 until it
  stops?

## p1Explain (one paragraph, under 80 wds; shown after the learner commits and runs)

- R3 becomes -184 minus -250, which is 66. Line 4 shows 66 on the display.
- Line 6 compares R3 (66) with R4 (100). 66 is less than 100, so it goes to line 9.
- Lines 7 and 8 never run, and CLASH stays dark. The machine runs lines 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 9.

## runLead (a lead above the figure: three short paragraphs, under 150 wds)

- This is the same machine and program, with its buttons. Each press of "Run one line" runs one
  line. The row marked "Next" is the line it runs next.
- Watch "The numbers it keeps": the number a line changes is marked "Changed". Watch "What the
  shop sees" after line 4.
- Press "Run one line" until the program stops. Then press "Start again".
- Now change room A's reading to -50 (room A's freezer is failing, at -5.0 degrees) and press
  "Run" to watch the whole program. "Pause" stops a run.
- Ask: which lines run this time, and what does the display show?

## runAfter (one paragraph, under 90 wds; shown once the learner has run a line)

- With the rooms at -184 and -250, each line changed at most one thing: R1 became -184, R2 -250,
  R3 66, R4 100, then the display showed 66.
- With room A at -50, R3 becomes 200. 200 is not less than 100, so line 6 does not go to line 9.
  Lines 7 and 8 run: R5 becomes 4 and CLASH lights.
- The program did not change. Only a reading changed, and the same lines gave a different
  display and a lit lamp.
