## Facts

- The capstone: room A reads -120 (-12.0 degrees) and room B -250. The machine is paused before
  line 3. R1 is -120, R2 is -250. The figure above the challenge shows the paused machine, with no
  buttons.
- The answers: line 3 changes R3 only; R3 becomes -120 minus -250 = 130; the next line is 4; the
  part that works out 130 is the part that adds.
- Lines 1 and 2 read the rooms: the rooms' readings reach the machine through its memory, so their
  numbers come from the memory. A line that works a number out, like line 3, gets it from the part
  that adds.
- The challenge's four fields: which number the line changes (R1 to R5, or none); its new number;
  the line the machine runs next; which part works the new number out (the memory, or the part that
  adds). The tests run the real machine one line on a copy and compare. There are 4 tests.
- The next module, Module 1 ("Voltage to numbers"), starts at the bottom of the ladder: one wire,
  and how its voltage becomes a number. Its first lesson asks how a voltage on a wire becomes a
  number.

## generalisation (two short paragraphs, under 130 wds)

- The ladder in this lesson is the course's plan. Each module builds one level from the level below
  and then uses it as a box: Module 1 a wire's voltage as a 1 or a 0, Module 2 the smallest parts,
  Module 3 a part that adds one place of two numbers, Module 7 the part that adds whole numbers, and
  Module 8 the machine. Later modules build the programs.
- A level hides the levels below it. You can use the part that adds without opening it, because it
  was tested. When something goes wrong, you open a level at a time until you find the wire.

## c2Hints.1 to c2Hints.5 (in this order)

2. The mistake: answering for the whole program. Trace line 3 only.
3. A smaller example: in the last lesson, with -184 and -250, line 3 set R3 to 66, then line 4 ran.

## reflection (two short paragraphs, under 110 wds)

- The machine is parts inside parts. One number at the top is 1s and 0s on wires at the bottom, and
  one stuck wire changes what the shop sees.
- The course climbs the ladder from the bottom. End with the question Module 1 starts from: how
  does a voltage on one wire become a 1 or a 0, and many of them a number?

## modelVsReality (two short paragraphs, under 130 wds)

- The drawings on this page are the circuit the simulator runs, opened a level at a time. A real
  chip is laid out by tools to fit a small area; its parts are not in the tidy rows the drawings
  show.
- A real wire's voltage is not simply high or low: it moves between them, and noise moves it.
  Module 1 starts there.
- A real fault like the stuck wire happens when a chip is damaged or made wrong. Chip makers test
  each chip for such faults before they sell it.
