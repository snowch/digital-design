question: Lessons 1 and 2 showed every level of the whole machine at one edge. The figures chose the edges and the paths.

Can you follow one value of your own choosing, at an edge of your own choosing, from a line of the program down to one gate?

The figure is paused before edge 40, the ALU edge of `word[lamps] <= R5` at `024`. Choose any other edge from the strip under the drawing.

motivation: Following one value from a line of the program, through every level, down to one gate is a **trace**. The figure gives you four tools for making one.

- The strip under the drawing pauses the run before any edge of any line.
- A wire you press stays pinned. It is marked wherever it is drawn as you open blocks, and its value shows under the drawing.
- The table of the levels you have opened records each block, its maker and its ports' values at that edge. Press a port in it to pin its wire.
- The list "Parts here that never open" opens the parts the simulator runs whole.

Those parts are the register file, the memory, the PC, the IR, the held words, the control registers, the controller's state register, and the selectors and adders that take a whole word. Each shows one bit, drawn as the module that built that kind of part drew one bit. A register's bit is drawn as Module 5 drew it. The register file's bit is drawn as one register's bit, Module 5's. A bit of a byte of the RAM is drawn as a register's bit, Module 5's. A selector's bit is drawn as Module 3 drew it, and an adder's as Module 3's full adder. Each part's inputs carry the machine's own values at that edge.

A part that only splits a word into its bits or joins bits into a word has no gate. The list of parts that never open says so, and the trace passes through it.

c1Task: 1. Each answer is a row of bits, read at a named edge of a line of the shop's program. Each row's boxes are numbered by the wires they hold, highest first. Use the figure under the challenge: pause at the edge, then trace. Set each bit with its button.
2. In the ALU's group `q0` (the slices for bits 3 to 0), at the ALU edge of `R3 <= R1 - R2`, at `010`, two rows:
   - the output of the XOR gate `xorB` in each slice, bits 3 to 0 (it turns B's bit over for a subtraction);
   - the carry out of each slice's full adder, bits 3 to 0.
3. At the ALU edge of `goto R15`, at `040`: D of the PC's bits 5 to 2, as Module 5's drawing shows them. `goto R15` takes FETCH, READ and ALU, and the PC takes its next value at the ALU edge.
4. At the WRITE edge of `R5 <= R3 >= R4 signed`, at `018`: EN of bit 0 of each of R7 to R0, as the register file's bit shows it.
5. There are 4 tests, one for each row.
