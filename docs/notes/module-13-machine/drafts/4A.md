In this lesson you write the top module of the whole machine, `machine`, in the course's SystemVerilog. It uses the subset Modules 8 to 10 used. No new construct is needed. The course supplies nine parts: `memory`, `decoder`, `system`, `controller`, `traplogic`, `cregs`, `registers`, `alu` and `condition`. You write the joins between them, and the small parts between them:

- the PC;
- the address the memory reads;
- the IR;
- the held words HA, HB, HR and HM;
- the constant made a word (WIDE);
- the ALU's two inputs;
- the word register Y takes;
- the target of a branch;
- the next PC.

The course's parts are not your own earlier answers. Each lesson keeps its own work. Each of the course's parts passes the tests your part passed in its module. The joins are yours.

Five programs test your machine. Each reaches a different part of the machine. Each runs on your machine and on the model, and the two are compared after every instruction and every trap:

- The shop's program: a set if, a call through a register, a system call and `resume`. 69 edges.
- A fault in user mode: the read of room A's sensor traps with cause `32`, because user mode refuses a device's address. 36 edges.
- A system call, then the timer: the timer runs out and interrupts the program with cause `81`. 53 edges.
- The door: the door opens while the program runs, and the program is interrupted with cause `82`. 31 edges.
- A store to the ROM: a store to `010` traps with cause `34`, and with no handler the machine halts. 7 edges.

A test fails at the first instruction or trap after which the two disagree. Its sentence names that line, its address, what differs, and the value on your machine and by the model. It does not name the join. Finding the join from that sentence is the skill this lesson teaches.
