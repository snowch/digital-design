## The control registers, for reading a failure (Module 12's words)

C0 holds the mode and interrupts on; at a trap it takes `01`, and at `resume` it takes C1. C1 takes
C0 at a trap. C2 takes the return point at a trap. C3 takes the cause at a trap. C4 holds the
handler's address, which the program writes. A failure naming C0 to C4 points at the control
registers and what they take in: the cause, TRAP, and `resume`'s signal.

### `generalisation` (about 110 words)

1. Lesson 1's "Testing the joins" said a part's tests cannot test its joins. Teams that design a CPU
   test them as this lesson did: the same programs on the design and on a model, compared after
   every instruction; many more programs than seven, many made at random; and they measure which
   parts the programs reached.
2. Programs that agree do not prove a design right: they show that no program reached a wrong part.
   The hidden text passed six of the seven programs.
