# Brief 3B: lesson `tracing` (Module 13, lesson 3), second part

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first and brief 3A's section on the figure; they apply
here. Do not use abstraction. You may use CPU, machine code, join and **trace** (lesson 3's plain
word for following one value down; not bold here).

Keys to write: `investigation`, `sumLead`, `sumAfter`, `construction`, `pcLead`,
`failureExperiment`, `faultLead`, `faultAlu`.

## investigation (about 60 words, above the figure)

Trace bit 1 of the ALU's result at the same edge, edge 28, from the datapath down to one gate, the
way Module 0's ladder went, but this time you open each block yourself.

## sumLead (a numbered list of steps)

1. Open `datapath`, then `alu`, then `g0`, then `q0`, then `bit1`: the slice for bit 1, Module 7's.
2. Open `fa`, Module 3's full adder, then `ha2`, the half adder that makes the sum.
3. Read the output of its XOR gate, and the last row of the table of levels.

## sumAfter (about 120 words)

1. The levels, from the top: the datapath (Module 8); the ALU (Module 7), whose output Y is
   `FFFFFFFFFFFFFFDE`, -34; its group of 16 bits, `g0`, and of 4, `q0`; the slice for bit 1; the
   full adder `fa` (Module 3); its half adder `ha2` (Module 3). Each level's output carries the same
   1 in bit 1, in its own form: a digit of a word at the top, one wire at the bottom.
2. At the bottom, the XOR gate `xorSum` gives SUM, 1.
3. The slice for bit 1 is called `bit1` inside its group of four. Further up the word, the names
   start again in each group: bit 6 of the word is `bit2` in group `q1`.

## construction (about 140 words, a numbered list after one sentence)

Trace the PC through a call through a register, at a part that never opens. The figure is paused
before edge 48, the WRITE edge of `call R6, R15`, at `02C`. Facts, in order:

1. The PC never opens. In "Parts here that never open", at the datapath's level, it shows bit 3 as
   Module 5's register bit with a reset: D is 0, EN is 1, RST is 0, and the flip-flop holds 1. The
   PC holds `02C`, whose bit 3 is 1, and takes `034`, whose bit 3 is 0. After the edge the bit
   holds 0.
2. D comes from the next PC's choice, Module 12's `nextTrap`. Open it: its two selectors, Module
   3's, both choose their first input. TRAP is 0 and RESUME is 0.
3. That input comes from `next`, Module 8's. Open it: the selector `pickJump` chooses RESULT,
   because JUMP is 1 for a call through a register.
4. RESULT is the ALU's: R6 plus the constant 0, `034`.

## pcLead (one sentence)

Show the PC's bit 3, then follow its D back through `nextTrap` and `next` to the ALU.

## failureExperiment (about 80 words, above the figure)

1. The figure holds one wire in the ALU at 0. Its label does not say which.
2. Under the drawing, the comparison with the model names the first instruction that disagrees.
3. Your task: trace down from what is wrong to the wire that is held. Start from the display and
   the comparison, read the result's bits, and open the ALU along the bit that differs.

## faultLead (one sentence)

Choose the fault, press "Run to the end", read the comparison, then trace.

## faultAlu (about 90 words; shown once the fault has run)

1. The comparison says: after `R3 <= R1 - R2` at `010`, R3 is 64 on the machine and 66 by the model.
   The display shows 64.
2. 66 is `42` in hexadecimal and 64 is `40`: only bit 1 differs.
3. The trace goes datapath, `alu`, `g0`, `q0`, `bit1`. There the wire SUM, out of the full adder, is
   drawn held at 0 while the full adder gives 1.
4. Every other instruction that uses the ALU's bit 1 adds or subtracts wrongly whenever that bit
   should be 1.
