# Brief 3L and 3D: lesson `tracing`, titles, labels and the challenge's sentences

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here. The labels may use CPU, machine
code, join and trace, and only the keys marked may use abstraction. A heading names what its
section contains, in a few words, with no full stop. An objective starts with a verb, is plain
text with no backticks, and ends with a full stop. A caption is one sentence describing the
figure, ending with a full stop.

## Labels (each one line)

- `title`: the lesson's question: can you follow one value from a line of a program down to one
  gate?
- `objectives.1` to `objectives.4`: pause the whole machine before any edge of any line; trace a
  value through every level down to a gate or a flip-flop; read a part that never opens as one bit
  of the module that built it; say what each level hides, and why the level above may ignore it
  (may use abstraction).
- `titles.question`: a path of your own. `titles.motivation`: three tools for a trace.
  `titles.prediction`: one bit of HR. `titles.investigation`: from the datapath to one gate.
  `titles.construction`: through the PC. `titles.failureExperiment`: a wire held at 0.
  `titles.explanation`: what each level hides. `titles.generalisation`: levels and boxes.
  `titles.challenge`: five traces. `titles.reflection`: joining the parts yourself.
- `challengeTitles.c1`: five traces.
- `captions.free`: the whole machine paused before the ALU edge of a set if, ready to trace.
- `captions.predict`: the same, with a question about one bit of HR.
- `captions.sum`: the same, for a trace of one bit of the ALU's result.
- `captions.pc`: the whole machine paused before the WRITE edge of a call through a register.
- `captions.fault`: the shop's program with one wire in the ALU held at 0.
- `captions.answers`: five wires to trace at named edges.
- `captions.tool`: the whole machine, for the challenge's traces.
- `faults.alu`: a wire in the ALU held at 0.
- `fields.xorB`: xorB in the slice for bit 2. `fields.sum1`: SUM in the slice for bit 1.
  `fields.pcD`: D of the PC's bit 3. `fields.r5En`: EN of R5's bit 0. `fields.r4En`: EN of R4's
  bit 0.

## The challenge's sentences for a wrong answer

Each key is one or two sentences shown when the learner's answer to one field is wrong. `{actual}`
stands for the learner's answer and must appear once, as given. Say which rule or which level the
wrong answer misses; never give the right bit.

- `traceXorB`: pause at the ALU edge of `R3 <= R1 - R2` and open the ALU down to the slice for bit
  2; the XOR turns B's bit over when the ALU subtracts.
- `traceSum1`: open the slice for bit 1 at that same edge, and read SUM where it leaves the full
  adder.
- `tracePcD`: pause at the WRITE edge of `call R6, R15`; show the PC's bit 3 from the list of parts
  that never open; D is the bit of the PC's next value.
- `traceR5En`: pause at the WRITE edge of `R5 <= R3 >= R4 signed`; show the register file's bit for
  R5; EN is 1 for the register Y names, at a WRITE edge.
- `traceR4En`: the same edge, the register file's bit for R4; only one register is written at an
  edge.
