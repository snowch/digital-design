- `l1.c1Hints.4` (part of the answer, no edge numbers): the steps each kind takes. A constant job

  WRITE. HB and STATUS leave the same block.
- `l1.c1Hints.5` (the whole answer): HB is driven by the datapath. WAITING is driven by the memory
  port. STATUS is driven by the datapath. CAUSEM is driven by the memory port. The IR takes
  `resume`'s word at edge 15, and the PC takes `010` at edge 17.

  not the edge at which the IR takes `resume`'s word. The IR takes an instruction's word at its

- `l1.joinPcEdge` (same rules): `{actual}` is not the edge at which the PC takes `010`. `resume`

- `l1.fields.pcEdge`: a field label, no full stop: the edge at which the PC takes `010`.

## Lesson 2 (`full-path`)

- `l2.motivation`: the whole motivation, about 330 words, replacing the old one. Facts, in order:
  1. Each level shows the same instruction in its own form. The first form is the line of text,
     such as `R3 <= R1 - R2`. An assembler, a program, turns each line into a 32-bit instruction
     before the machine runs. Here the line becomes `13123000`.
  2. You have seen these words since Module 8: in the ROM, and in the IR. They are the program's
     **machine code**: the words the assembler makes from the lines, the form the machine runs.
     (Bold the term once, plain meaning first.) The ROM holds the shop's program as machine code;
     the machine never sees the lines.
  3. The IR holds the instruction being run. Its eight hexadecimal digits are its fields: K, J, A,
     B, Y and the constant. In `13123000`, K is 1, a register job. J is 3, subtract. A is R1, B is
     R2 and Y is R3. The constant `000` is unused.
  4. The decoder turns the fields into control signals. For a subtraction, OP2, OP1 and OP0 are
     `011`. WRITEY is 1, and SET is 0.
  5. The controller's state says which step the next edge makes. Keep this list exactly:
     - fetch: the FETCH edge, where the IR takes the word;
     - decode: the gates of the decoder, from the moment the IR holds the word; it takes no edge of
       its own;
     - register read: the READ edge, where HA and HB take registers A and B;
     - ALU: the ALU edge, where HR takes the result;
     - memory: the MEMORY edge, for a load or a store;
     - register write: the WRITE edge, where the register Y names takes its word;
     - PC update: the instruction's last edge, where the PC takes its next value; then the next
       instruction's FETCH.
  6. Not every instruction passes every state. `R3 <= R1 - R2` takes FETCH, READ, ALU and WRITE:
     four edges, and no MEMORY.
- `l2.c1Task.2`: item 2 of the task. Facts: the line `R7 <= R5 | R6` sits at `01C` in a program no
  figure runs. When it runs, R5 holds 12 and R6 holds 10. Work out, without running anything:
  how many edges it takes, from its FETCH edge to its last; the ALU's job at its ALU edge, by OP2
  OP1 OP0: add, subtract, copy B or OR; the word register Y takes at its WRITE edge, as a decimal
  number; the PC after its last edge, as three hexadecimal digits.
- `l2.c1Hints.1`: a word's eight digits are its fields in order: K, J, A, B, Y, then three digits
  of constant. The other answers follow from the states a register job takes and from its job
  digit.
- `l2.c1Hints.2`: a common mistake: counting a MEMORY edge for a line that neither loads nor
  stores.
- `l2.c1Hints.3`: a smaller example, no part of the task: `R4 <= 100` is `25004064`: kind 2, job 5,
  A 0, B 0, Y 4, constant `064`.
- `l2.c1Hints.4`: part of the answer: set if is kind A. `|` is a register job's OR, job 4. A
  register job's ALU code is its job digit.
- `l2.c1Hints.5`: the whole answer: `A6345000`; 4 edges; OR; 14; `020`.
- `l2.pathJob` (`{actual}` once, in backticks as given; never the job): `{actual}` is not the
  ALU's job at its ALU edge. A register job's ALU code, OP2 OP1 OP0, is its job digit, and the
  line's operator names the job.
- `l2.pathYin` (same rules): `{actual}` is not the word Y takes. Y takes the ALU's result: work
  out R5 and R6 bit by bit with the job, and give the word in decimal.
- `l2.fields.edges`: a field label: the edges that `R7 <= R5 | R6` takes.
