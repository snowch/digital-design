# Brief C1: the cover's path through the course, and its live circuit

You are drafting words for the cover at the top of the front page of an interactive course,
*Digital Design: From Bits to a Working Computer*. A reader meets the cover before every lesson,
including before the first, so it must not use any word that a lesson introduces as a term (list
below). Read `docs/style.md` in the repository before you write: it is the style checklist, and it
applies to every sentence. Use only the facts here. Where a sentence seems to need a fact you were
not given, write a note in square brackets instead of inventing it. Do not write or change any
file.

## Voice

British English. Direct, plain, active, short. The reader is "you". No marketing tone, no
slogans, no filler, no em dashes or en dashes used as dashes. "Press" for buttons, never "click"
or "tap". Numbers as digits.

## Words the cover must not use

These are terms a lesson introduces; the cover comes before them. Do not use them, nor their
plurals: bit, gate, truth table, Boolean expression, XOR, XNOR, NAND, universal, threshold, noise
margin, one-hot, carry, half adder, full adder, overflow, multiplexer, bus, decoder, demultiplexer,
encoder, comparator, depth, feedback, latch, transparent, synchronous, register, shift register,
register transfer, register file, counter, state machine, state diagram, next-state logic,
address, RAM, ROM, byte, aligned, alignment, memory-mapped, ALU, flag, instruction, program
counter, fetch, datapath, branch, control unit, illegal instruction, instruction register,
micro-operation, SystemVerilog. "AND", "OR" and "NOT" may be used, as the module names do.

## 1. The path through the course

Under the button that starts the course, a row of five stages shows the order the course takes,
each with a short name, a line under it, and the modules it covers (the page adds "Modules 1 to
3" and so on itself). Stages whose modules have no lessons yet are marked "still to be written"
by the page.

Return these keys:

- `journeyHeading`: a heading of at most six words over the row: the course's path, in order,
  from the first module to the last.
- `stage1Name`, `stage1About` ... `stage5Name`, `stage5About`: each name one to three words; each
  "about" one short line of at most twelve words, no full stop needed.

The facts for each stage:

1. Modules 1 to 3 (named "Voltage to numbers", "Learning AND, OR, NOT", "Selecting, comparing,
   adding"): two voltages on a wire become numbers; the first circuits combine signals with AND,
   OR and NOT; then circuits that choose one signal of several, compare two numbers, and add.
2. Modules 4 to 6 ("Memory and time", "Counting and sequences", "Accessing many numbers"):
   circuits that keep a value until told to change it; circuits that count and step through a
   sequence; a memory that keeps many numbers, each found by its own number.
3. Modules 7 to 9 ("Arithmetic and logic", "Putting pieces together", "Control and sequencing"):
   a circuit that does arithmetic and logic on whole numbers; the parts joined into one machine
   that runs a program; the control that takes the machine through each step.
4. Modules 10 and 11 ("The machine's vocabulary", "Programming and debugging"): the words the
   machine understands, and why they are as they are; writing programs for it and finding their
   mistakes.
5. Modules 12 and 13 ("Errors and responses", "The whole machine"): what the machine does when a
   program goes wrong; the finished machine, running programs from start to end.

## 2. The live circuit beside the opening words

Beside the course's opening paragraph sits a drawing of a real circuit from Module 2: the alarm
for a freezer room in the course's running story of a shop. Its inputs are WARM and DOOR, its
output ALARM. ALARM is 1 when the room is warm (WARM is 1) and the door is shut (DOOR is 0); with
the door open (DOOR is 1), ALARM is 0, so staff can load warm stock without setting it off. The
reader can press WARM and DOOR in the drawing to change them, and ALARM follows. The course's
simulator runs this drawing itself, as every lesson's drawings are run: it is not an animation.

Return these keys:

- `heroTitle`: the drawing's name, at most six words, read by a screen reader as the figure's
  title.
- `heroCaption`: at most two short sentences under the drawing: what it is, and that the reader
  can press WARM and DOOR to see ALARM change.

Return each key in the form `key: text`, one per line, with a blank line between keys, and
nothing else.
