# Brief N1: the heading over the note on how a page differs from real hardware

Write one heading. Facts only; the wording is yours. Follow `docs/style.md` (attached).

## Where it sits

1. Every lesson page ends with a short note, after the reflection, on how what the page shows
   differs from a real machine. The note has a heading.
2. On every page with a clocked figure the heading is "How the simulator differs from hardware".
   That heading stays as it is.
3. This brief is for the heading on the four pages with no clocked figure. Today it reads "How the
   model differs from hardware".

## The four notes it heads

4. 0.1, "What does a computer do?", the course's first lesson: the figures run the machine in the
   course's simulator; the degrees in the text are the machine's tenths; a real computer runs many
   millions of lines a second; a real sensor's reading changes on its own.
5. 0.2, "What is the machine made of?": the drawings show the circuit the simulator runs; a real
   chip is laid out by tools and its parts are not in tidy rows; a real wire's voltage is pushed
   about by nearby wires and motors.
6. 1.1, the signals lesson: the recordings are made by the course's model from a fixed starting
   number, the same on every visit; real noise differs.
7. 10.4, room to grow: the course's ROM holds 0s after a program; a real flash memory, erased and
   not written, reads all 1s.

## What the heading must do

8. Be true of all four notes. Notes 4 and 5 are about the simulator; note 6 is about a model of a
   signal, not the simulator; note 7 is about the course's memory. So it names neither "the
   simulator" nor "the model".
9. Be read first by someone on the course's first page, who knows binary numbers and everyday
   arithmetic and nothing about electronics or computers' insides. "Model" means nothing to them
   yet. "Hardware" and "real" are everyday words.
10. Be a label, as every heading in the course is: somebody scanning the page can tell what the
    section contains. Short, in the same shape as the other heading, "How the simulator differs
    from hardware".
11. British English. No em dashes. No full stop.

Return the heading alone, and nothing else.
