explanation: Each call of `warmRooms` that finds a room pushes 4 words. The first is the address its `goto R15` will return to. The other three are the words R10, R11 and R12 held when it was called. Those three belong to the call that made it: that call's room address, its limit and its count so far.

So a call that has not yet returned keeps its room and its count in the words pushed by the call it made. The stack view shows those words in that later call's group, one group nearer the top. The top group is the call running now.

When a call returns, it pops its four words, restoring R15 and R10 to R12. The call that made it then goes on with its own room and count.

The registers are shared by every call. The stack keeps, for each call not yet returned, what that call will need when it goes on.

modelVsReality: Real records that lead two ways, rooms or anything else, are kept as this lesson keeps them: each one holds the addresses of the next.

A real program that walks such records usually keeps a list of the records still to visit. It does this rather than calling itself, when the way in can be long. A call's words on the stack cost more than an address on a list.
