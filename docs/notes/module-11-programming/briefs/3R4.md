# Brief 3R4: lesson `functions` (Module 11, lesson 3), keys the reading review changes

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here. You may use every term up to
and including lesson 3's (not in bold). Never write "stack" or "frame": lesson 4 introduces the
stack.

Keys to write: `c1Task`, `c1Hints.2`, `c2Task`.

## c1Task (about 130 words, short paragraphs or a list)

1. The starting text is a main program that calls `overBy` once, for room A, and shows the result.
   The tests add `overBy` after your program; "The tests add these lines" shows it.
2. The tests' `overBy` gives the lesson's result, and then changes every register the calling
   convention lets a function change: R0 and R2 to R9. Only R1, its result, and R10 to R15 come
   back as they were.
3. Change the main program so that the display shows the larger of two amounts: room A's above
   -180, and room B's above -200. Then it stops.
4. Call `overBy` once for each room.
5. There are 5 tests. Each runs the program with a pair of readings, room A's then room B's:
   -170 and -190; -160 and -195; -175 and -170; -190 and -210; 25 and -150. Each checks the
   display, that two calls of `overBy` returned, and that the program ends at its `stop`.

## c1Hints.2 (one or two sentences, starting "A common mistake:")

A common mistake: keeping room A's result in a free register, such as R5, or in R2 to R4. The tests'
`overBy` changes R0 and R2 to R9 before it returns, as the calling convention allows, so the result
is lost.

## c2Task (about 170 words, short paragraphs)

1. The shop's fridge must stay between 2.0 and 5.0 degrees: readings 20 to 50. Its sensor is the one
   programs read as `sensorA`.
2. The starting text has a main program that calls `outOfRange` with the fridge's reading in R1, 20
   in R2 and 50 in R3, shows the result on the display, and turns ALARM on when the result is not 0.
   Its `outOfRange` gives 0 every time.
3. Write `outOfRange`: R1 holds a reading, R2 the range's low end, R3 its high end. Its result, in
   R1: how far the reading is outside the range (the low end minus the reading below it, the reading
   minus the high end above it), and 0 inside it. A reading equal to an end is inside.
4. Keep the calling convention: if you change any of R10 to R13, put it back before you return;
   leave R14 as it was (lesson 4 gives it its role); return with `goto R15`.
5. There are 10 tests. Seven call `outOfRange` alone. Before each, the test puts its own words in
   R10 to R13 and in R14, and in R15 the address of a `stop` it adds after your program, where the
   call returns. R1, R2 and R3 are: 60, 20, 50; 10, 20, 50; 30, 20, 50; 20, 20, 50; 50, 20, 50;
   -15, 20, 50; 75, 0, 40. Each checks R1, that R10 to R14 are as they were, and that the call
   returned through R15. A function that runs past its last line does not count as returned.
6. Three run the whole program with the fridge at 60, 30 and -5. Each checks the display, the lamps
   and that the program ends at its `stop`.
