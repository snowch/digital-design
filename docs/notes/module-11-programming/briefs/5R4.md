## explanation (about 130 words, short paragraphs)

1. Each call of `warmRooms` that finds a room pushes 4 words: the address its `goto R15` will
   return to, and the words R10, R11 and R12 held when it was called. Those three words belong to
   the call that made it: that call's room address, its limit and its count so far.
2. So a call that has not yet returned keeps its room and its count in the words the next call in
   pushed. The stack view shows them in that call's group, one group nearer the top; the top group
   is the call running now.
3. When a call returns, it pops those words back into R10 to R12, and the call that made it goes on
   with its own room and count.
4. The registers are shared by every call; the stack keeps, for each call not yet returned, what it
   will need when it goes on.
5. Do not repeat that a last case must come (the generalisation says it).

## modelVsReality (about 60 words)

1. Real records that lead two ways, rooms or anything else, are kept as this lesson keeps them: each
   holds the addresses of the next.
2. A real program walking such records usually keeps its own list of the records still to visit,
   rather than calling itself, when the way in can be long: a call's words on the stack cost more
   than an address on a list.
3. Do not repeat what lesson 4's note said about a stack growing too far, or the failure
   experiment's result.
