# Brief 4R5: lesson `stack` (Module 11, lesson 4), keys the reading review changes

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) and brief `4RB.md` (the lesson's program) first. You may
use every term of lessons 1 to 4 (not in bold). Never write "frame".

The construction's program has a function `sumKept` (renamed from `check`). It is called from `00C`,
so its return address is `010`. It pushes R15, R10, R11 and R12, in that order, before its first
call of `overBy`, and pops them in the opposite order before `goto R15`. The lesson lists it and
never runs it.

Keys to write: `generalisationFirst`, `c1Task`, `c1Hints.1` to `c1Hints.5`, `c2Task`,
`fields.r11At`, `fields.r12At`, `fields.inOverBy`, `fields.leftAt7B8`.

## generalisationFirst (about 50 words, the first paragraph of the generalisation)

1. A call made inside another starts later and returns sooner: the inner function returns before
   the outer one goes on.
2. The stack matches that order. The last word pushed is the first popped, so the inner call's
   words sit on top of the outer call's, and come off first.
3. Do not mention the failure experiment or its cause.

## c1Task (about 80 words, a list allowed)

1. In a run of the program with `sumKept`, with room A at -170 and room B at -190, give in
   hexadecimal:
   - the address of the word that holds what R11 held when `sumKept` started;
   - the address of the word that holds what R12 held;
   - what R14 holds while `overBy` runs;
   - the word at `7B8` once `sumKept` has returned to the main program.
2. Write each as three hexadecimal digits. There are 4 tests, one for each answer.

## c1Hints (five, in this order, each starting with its label)

1. The idea: start R14 at `7C0` and take 8 off for each push, in the order the pushes run.
2. A common mistake: thinking a pop clears the word it takes. A pop copies the word into a register
   and adds 8 to R14. The word stays in the RAM until a push writes over it.
3. A smaller example: in the lesson's run, `sumOver` pushes R15 to `7B8` and R10 to `7B0`.
4. Part of the answer: `sumKept` pushes R15 to `7B8` and R10 to `7B0`, then R11 and R12. `overBy`
   pushes nothing.
5. The whole answer: `7A8`, `7A0`, `7A0` and `010`.

## c2Task (about 170 words, a numbered list)

1. Write the function `roomsOver`. R1 holds room A's reading and R2 room B's. Its result, in R1: how
   many rooms are above their limits, 0, 1 or 2. Room A's limit is -180 and room B's is -200. A
   reading equal to its limit is not above it.
2. Use the function `overBy`, which the starting text gives. Its result is 0 when a reading is not
   above its limit. Call it once for each room.
3. Keep the calling convention: R10 to R14 as they were when `roomsOver` returns, and return through
   R15.
4. The starting text's main program sets R14, calls `roomsOver` and shows the result. Its
   `roomsOver` gives 0.
5. There are 9 tests. Six call `roomsOver` alone. Before each, the test puts its own words in R10 to
   R13, `7C0` in R14, and in R15 the address of a `stop` it adds after your program, where the call
   returns. R1 and R2 are: -170 and -190; -185 and -210; -170 and -210; -185 and -190; 25 and 30;
   -180 and -200. Each checks R1, that two calls of `overBy` returned, that the stack held at least
   3 words, that R10 to R14 are as they were, and that the call returned through R15.
6. Three run the whole program with room A and room B at -170 and -190; -185 and -210; -170 and
   -210. Each checks the display and that the program ends at its `stop`.
