nowPrevExplorerAfter: One wire, from the NOW register's Q to the PREV register's D, carries the word between them. Nothing else is needed to make PREV wait one save behind NOW. The edge does it, since both registers take their D at the same moment.

predictLongPressLead: The circuit above has a flaw that the tests you ran did not catch. A person keeps Save pressed for far longer than one clock period. SAVE stays 1 for many edges, not just one. This figure saves `0011`, sets IN to `0101`, and then keeps SAVE pressed for three edges. Watch what happens to PREV.

p2Question: A save of `0011` happened. Then IN is `0101` and SAVE is kept pressed for three edges. What is PREV after the third edge?

p2Explain: PREV is `0101`. The first edge of the press: NOW took `0101`, PREV took `0011` (NOW's old value). The second edge: NOW took `0101` again, PREV took NOW's word, which was `0101` by then. The number `0011` is lost. The clock does not wait for your finger.

saveOnceLead: The fix uses a flip-flop called last. At every edge, last takes SAVE, and its output OLD shows what SAVE was at the previous edge. Two gates combine these: notOld produces NOT OLD, and andStep produces STEP = SAVE AND NOT OLD. STEP is 1 only before the first edge of a press, because after that edge, OLD becomes 1 and STEP becomes 0. STEP replaces SAVE as the enable for both registers. Try it: press SAVE to 1, then press Clock CLK three times. Release SAVE and press Clock CLK. Then press SAVE to 1 again to save a new value.

saveOnceAfter: After the first edge of the press, NOW is `0101` and PREV is `0011`. The next two edges change nothing. A new press saves again. The flip-flop last stores one bit: SAVE's value from the edge before. That bit becomes OLD, and it is what makes STEP turn off after the first edge of the press.
