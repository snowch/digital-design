# Review: counters (reviewer subagent, returned as text, saved by the managing model)

Review of `counters` (served on 4191, walked at 1280 and 375 px, server stopped). `npx vitest run content/lessons/counters.facts.test.ts` passes (6 tests). I took screenshots of every section at both widths and checked the page statically. I did not press the controls or run the challenges with a wrong attempt. The page showed no horizontal page scroll and no console errors, apart from one 404 for a resource at 1280 px (not identified).

1. F1. Category: label. Severity: medium. Confidence: high. Section: Construction. Three restatements of one task stack up: the section prose "Build a 2-bit counter from the parts offered...", then the lead "Draw a 2-bit counter. The parts are "D flip-flop with reset" blocks and half adder blocks.", then the challenge task "Draw a circuit with inputs EN, RST and CLK...". Direction: say each fact once. The prose line "See how bit 1 depends on bit 0's carry to compute its next value." is an instruction nobody can act on, and it half-gives the build. The lead appears below the prose instead of above the figure.

2. F2. Category: term. Severity: medium. Confidence: medium. Section: Construction and the c1 hints. The "D flip-flop with reset" block is used as if met. The registers lesson built its reset from gates (a reset gate and a load-enable bit). The learner has never been told this part exists or what its RST pin does (a reset that acts only at an edge). Direction: one clause where the part first appears, saying what its RST does.

3. F3. Category: fact. Severity: medium. Confidence: medium. Section: Explanation, "add one" figure. The lead says "Q is set to `0111` and EN to 0, so NEXT is `0111`. Press EN to 1. The status line says "Settled in 5 steps."" At load, with EN 0, the figure already shows "Step 5 of 5" and "Settled in 5 steps." (screenshots), before the learner presses anything. The sentence therefore does not tell the learner anything new. The slider and status line also seem to describe a run that has not happened. Direction: check what the initial status should be, or say what the learner will see after pressing EN.

4. F4. Category: figure. Severity: medium. Confidence: high. Section: Explanation, phone (375 px). The "add one" drawing is wider than the screen: "The drawing is wider than the screen. Scroll sideways to see the rest." The lesson's key picture, the carry travelling through ha0 to ha3, shows only ha0 on a phone and cuts off at the right edge. The prose says "Each step, the carry reaches one more half adder", which a phone learner cannot see. Direction: fit the drawing to the width or give it a narrower layout.

5. F5. Category: arc. Severity: medium. Confidence: medium. Section: Explanation. The opening prose says "The figure below shows this." and the lead then begins "The figure shows the add one block alone..." (a repeat). The explanation also repeats the investigation: "The half adders work out a new NEXT" and the explorer's own account of the chain. The section's real new point, that NEXT passes through `0110`, `0100` and `0000` before it settles and the register never takes them, sits in the "after" text. Direction: keep it, and trim the earlier repeats.

6. F6. Category: double meaning. Severity: medium. Confidence: high. Section: Question, Motivation, Generalisation and the TICK explanations. TICK has three descriptions. "A lamp, TICK, lights when the count is about to start again" is true only while EN is 1 (c2 task: "TICK is 1 while the count is `1111` and EN is 1"). The `counterExplorerAfter` text has it as "the last half adder's carry", and the Generalisation text says "EQ is the TICK here", so TICK is `0101` there. "About to start again" is also not the wait itself: TICK is high at `1111` and the wait ends at the next edge. Direction: say once that TICK is the last carry, and note that it depends on EN.

7. F7. Category: double meaning. Severity: low. Confidence: medium. Section: whole lesson. "keep" and "hold" are used for the register's behaviour with EN 0. "EN is the load enable from the registers lesson" (p2Explain) and "counts at an edge only while EN is 1" give EN two roles, load and count. The c1 task says "they keep their current values". Direction: keep one verb for what a register does when EN is 0. "Load" is also used for "loads `0000`" in the Generalisation lead, which is a reset by EQ, not a load enable.

8. F8. Category: arc. Severity: medium. Confidence: medium. Section: Prediction. The answer options place the correct option first in p1 ("Q is 0000") and p2 ("Q is 0010"), and p3's third option is `XXXX`. A learner sees the right answer in the first slot twice in a row. The p1 intro text also says the figure "resets the circuit, so Q is `0000`", which does not give the answer. Direction: vary the option order.

9. F9. Category: style. Severity: low. Confidence: high. Section: Prediction. "Two figures run the circuit the question asks for: a 4-bit register and an "add one" block." This sentence is a label. Direction: say what each figure does. Also, the Failure experiment's "What happens if the counter never gets reset? The circuit is drawn above the question." puts the figure's caption in the wrong place.

10. F10. Category: fact. Severity: low. Confidence: medium. Section: Failure experiment, fault lab. The lead says "ha1's CARRY into ha2" and "ha0's XOR". The fault label reads "C2 forced to 0", and the lead calls the net the "wire carrying ha1's CARRY into ha2". The learner has to open the block to find ha0 to ha3 and "C2". The name "C2" appears only in the fault label. Direction: use the same name in prose and label.

11. F11. Category: arc. Severity: medium. Confidence: medium. Section: Failure experiment. The three outcomes in `counterFaultsOutcomes` explain each fault with full counts ("0000, 0001, 0010, 0011, then back to 0000, 0001"). The lead tells the learner what a healthy count looks like but only asks for a prediction. Nothing says to predict the fault's count, only "which checks will fail". Direction: make clear what is to be predicted. The section title "Three faults and no reset" mixes two figures, and the no-reset prediction is the one that fits "explanation" (p3Explain says "A counter needs a reset before it can count").

12. F12. Category: figure. Severity: low. Confidence: medium. Section: Generalisation. "EQ, ORed with RST, drives the register's RST." The figure draws the OR as "orClear", while the prose says "ORed" and "RST" for both the input and the register's RST pin, so one name covers two wires. "Module 3's comparator" is introduced by "The figure adds Module 3's comparator. It compares Q with the fixed word `0101`." The "last" constant block is not named in the prose. Direction: name the constant, and use "the register's RST pin" and "RST input" differently.

13. F13. Category: arc. Severity: medium. Confidence: medium. Section: Construction and Challenge hints. The c1 hints do not climb one rung at a time. Hint 1 already says "A half adder gives both the SUM and CARRY you need". Hint 2 warns against sending EN to both half adders. Hints 4 and 5 then spell out the full wiring and repeat each other. c2 hint 2, "A TICK taken from an AND of the four Q outputs alone stays 1 at an edge where EN is 0", already gives the design away; the test fails the AND of Q alone, and the same fact is in the task text ("TICK is 1 while the count is `1111` and EN is 1"). Also c1 hint 3 says "put Q0 on half adder A and EN on its B", which is a pin-level instruction before the learner has tried anything. Direction: separate the ladder.

14. F14. Category: arc. Severity: medium. Confidence: low. Section: Challenge. A learner doing the challenge from the lesson alone has what they need for the 4-bit counter, but "TICK" in the question is a lamp driven by "the last half adder's carry" only in the explorer's "after" text. The Challenge section carries no prose of its own, only the lead "Draw it from the same parts as the construction" and a task that says TICK changes "as soon as EN or the count changes, not only at an edge" (which ties to TICK being combinational, never explained earlier). Direction: say in the lesson that TICK is a wire out of the adder, not a register output.

15. F15. Category: style. Severity: low. Confidence: medium. Section: Motivation and Investigation. "Feed the register's own Q into an adder that adds 1" followed by "Bit 0's half adder adds EN instead". The counter adds EN, not 1; with EN 1 it adds 1. "Adds 1" and "adds EN" conflict until the second paragraph. "The loop does not run wild" is a performance of the point. Direction: say that EN is the 1 being added.

16. F16. Category: look. Severity: low. Confidence: medium. Section: Question. The scene's box is marked "?" and the lesson says "Count edges while the switch is on", but the learner cannot see what the box does and the lamp reads "Lamp", with no tie to TICK in the figure other than the wire label. Direction: fine as a black box, but name the lamp and display consistently with the prose ("Display" vs "4-bit display").

17. F17. Category: originality. Severity: low. Confidence: medium. Section: whole lesson. A counter built as a register plus incrementer (and a count-to-5 reset) is close to the standard textbook presentation, but the shop's wait, the half-adder chain and the stepped-carry figure make it the course's own. No change needed.

## Overall
The lesson holds the predict, build, run, break, explain, generalise loop and its facts test passes. The main faults are repetition in the Construction section, hints that do not climb, TICK described three ways, and a phone view that cuts off the one figure that shows the carry spreading. The "D flip-flop with reset" part is used before it is introduced. Fix F4, F6, F1 and F13 first. The rest is polish.
