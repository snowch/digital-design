# Brief W6: lesson `nesting` (lesson 6), after the second reading

Read `00-module.md` first, including both "Decisions" sections. You may use **trap**, **handler**,
**user mode**, **system mode**, **system call** and **interrupt**, not bold. Each key replaces the
page's text. Write each from its facts alone.

Keys: `lateLead`, `savedAfter`, `c2Task`, label `waitLabels.3`.

## lateLead (numbered steps; about 70 words)

1. Press "Run to a breakpoint". The run pauses at the handler's door part. Read how many
   instructions the status line says ran, and the panel "The timer and the door".
2. The status line says 172. The door's interrupt itself came after 167 instructions; the
   handler's first five lines, `01C` to `02C`, which save R8 and R9 and choose the door part by
   C3, ran between the interrupt and the pause.
3. Press "Run to the end".

## savedAfter (about 60 words; shown once a run has ended, whichever door time was chosen)

Say only what holds for every choice. The status line gives each run's own count of instructions
and traps; do not list them.

1. Whatever the choice, the program shows 5, then 6, and the handler's `stop` for job 4 ends the
   run.
2. The word at `418` holds `10C`, the return point job 5 saved.
3. The word at `410` holds 2: C1's two bits, `10`, read as a number.
4. The status line says how many instructions ran and how many traps went to the handler; compare
   it across the choices.

## c2Task (a numbered list)

1. The starting text holds the start and the whole handler. The handler offers job 1, job 4 and a
   job of its own, job 6: job 6 shows R2 on the display, then R2 minus 1, and so on down to 1. It
   counts with interrupts off.
2. The start runs the program with the status a word in each test's lines gives, named
   `startStatus`: `10` (user mode, interrupts on) in three tests, `00` (user mode, interrupts off)
   in one.
3. Change job 6 so that the door and the timer can come in while it counts, and the program goes
   on after it as before.
4. A system call may change only R1 and R2. Job 6 must leave every other register as the program
   left it, and give the program back its status.
5. There are 4 tests. Each adds a user program after yours, named `program`. Each puts a word of
   its own in R0 and in each of R3 to R15, a different set in each test, asks job 6 to count down
   from 20 or 30, and ends with job 4. Two also show 5 before the count and 6 after it. Each test
   opens the door at its own time, or not at all.
6. Each test checks the words the display showed, in order; the lamps; C1 at the end, the
   program's status at its last trap, which must be the status its `startStatus` gave; R0 and R3
   to R15 as the program left them at its last trap, the call for job 4; and that the run ends at
   the handler's `stop` for job 4.
