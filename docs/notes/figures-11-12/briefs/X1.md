# Brief X1: Module 11's figures pass (lessons 2 to 5)

Read `docs/style.md` first. Module 11's terms each lesson may use: lesson 2 `lists`: breakpoint
(introduced in its investigation; the logLead comes before it, so not there); lesson 3
`functions`: function, arguments, calling convention; lesson 4 `stack`: stack; lesson 5
`recursion`: recursion. No term from Module 12. Write each key from its facts alone. A key
marked "(added)" is a new sentence or step that goes at the end of the existing text: write only
the new words. Keep lists as lists.

## lesson `lists` (2)

- `logLead.replace` — this replaces one sentence of the question's lead: "It shows R1, and marks
  which reading in the log R1 points to." Facts: the figure draws the log's words as boxes at
  their addresses (`038` count, then the readings from `040`), and an arrow from R1 points at
  the word whose address R1 holds. One or two sentences.
- `investigation.replace` — replaces the last sentence of the investigation: "A view of the log
  shows its six words and marks the one whose address a register holds." Facts: a drawing of the
  log shows seven words as boxes: the six readings and the word after them, at `070`. An arrow
  from each register that holds a word's address points at that word. The box of a word the last
  step stored has a thick outline. Two short sentences.
- `walkLead.replace` — replaces the clause "and the log view marks the word R1 has moved on to"
  at the end of a sentence that begins "The watch shows it as what `word[R1]` "was"," Write only
  the replacing clause, starting "and": R1's arrow in the drawing of the log points at the word R1
  has moved on to.
- `walkAfter` (added): after the last reading, at `068`, R1 moves on to `070`, the word after the
  log, and its arrow points there when the run stops. One sentence.
