## question (about 70 words)

   signals from K and J alone?" Quote it.
2. In Module 8's first two lessons you set WRITEY and BCONST by hand for each instruction.
3. From Module 8's third lesson the decoder set every control signal, drawn closed.
4. End on the question: which gates turn the kind and the job into the twelve control signals?

## motivation (about 100 words)

1. The part of a machine that works out its control signals is its **control unit**. Introduce
   the term here, plain meaning first.
2. In Module 8's machine the control unit is the decoder alone. Every instruction takes one edge,
   so its signals depend only on its own digits, K and J.
3. Each kind needs its own set of signals. A load writes register Y, reads memory and gives the
   ALU the constant. A branch writes no register and subtracts.
4. Some signals follow the job digit: for a register job, OP2, OP1 and OP0 are the job's bits 2,
   1 and 0.

## p1Question (one sentence)

For a jump, kind 7, what does the decoder set BCONST to?

## p1Explain (about 70 words)

1. BCONST is 1.
2. A jump goes to RA + c. The ALU works that out: A takes RA, B takes the constant, and the ALU
   adds.
3. So the decoder sets BCONST to 1, and OP2 OP1 OP0 to `010`, add.
4. With BCONST at 0, B would take RB, and the jump would go to RA + RB.
5. A jump writes no register: WRITEY is 0. JUMP is 1, so the next PC is the ALU's result.

## decoderOpenLead (above the investigation's figure; about 140 words, a list allowed)

1. The figure is the decoder opened. It is Module 8's decoder, which also takes C, the
   constant. Only its checks read C; the next lesson opens them.
2. Its blocks:

   - "split" splits J into its bits J3, J2, J1 and J0.
   - "control signals" works out the twelve signals from the kind lines and the job's bits.
   - "checks" works out STOP and CAUSED.
3. Press a block to open it. Press K's and J's pins to change the instruction.
4. The figure starts with a subtract: kind 1, job 3.

## decoderOpenAfter (below the figure; about 150 words, a list allowed)

1. Inside "kind lines" are two of Module 3's 2-to-4 decoders. One reads K3 and K2, the other K1
   and K0. An AND gate per kind joins one line from each. Kind 5, `0101`, is line 1 of the first
   AND line 1 of the second.
2. A kind line that is a control signal on its own carries the signal's name: kind 3's line is
   LOAD, kind 4's STORE, kind 5's BRANCH, kind 6's CALL, kind 7's JUMP. The lines of kinds 1, 2
   and 8 are KIND1, KIND2 and KIND8.
3. Inside "control signals", each other signal is an OR of the kind lines that need it.
   - JOBS is KIND1 OR KIND2: the two kinds of job. MEM is LOAD OR STORE.
   - WRITEY is an OR of JOBS, LOAD and CALL.
   - Where a signal follows the job, an AND gate joins a line with a job bit: OP2 is JOBS AND
     J2.
4. For the subtract, KIND1 and JOBS are 1, WRITEY is 1, and OP2 OP1 OP0 are `011`, J's low bits.
