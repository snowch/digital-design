# Brief 5B: lesson `new-instruction` (Module 9, lesson 5), construction and faults

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here. Every Module 9 term is
allowed.

Keys to write: `construction`, `writeDecoderLead`, `c1Task`, `c1Hints.1` to `c1Hints.5`,
`callFaultsLead`, `callFaultsOutcomes`.

## construction (about 100 words, a list allowed)

1. The new instruction needs no new part of the datapath:
   - "word for Y" already gives PC + 4 when CALL is 1;
   - the next PC is already the ALU's result when JUMP is 1;
   - the ALU already adds RA and the constant for a jump.
2. It needs no change to the controller. It sets CALL, so it takes the call's edges.
3. The work is in the decoder:
   - a column of signals for kind 9: the call's and the jump's together;
   - the checks: kind 9, job 0 is now an instruction, and kind 9's other jobs are illegal.

## c1Task (about 140 words, a list allowed)

1. The text is the whole decoder, from lessons 1 and 2. It has inputs K, J and C, and outputs
   the twelve control signals, MEM (a load or a store), STOP and CAUSED.
2. Make three changes:
   - add an arm `4'h9:` to the `case`, setting the signals kind 9 needs;
   - in ILLEGAL's first term, stop refusing kind 9;
   - in the calls' and jumps' term, refuse kind 9 with any job but 0.
3. The tests give every kind and job with the constant 0, jobs 2 and 3 of kind 8 with three
   constants, and kind 9 with the constant `008`: 263 tests.
4. The start fails 17 of them. The first is "K 9, J 0".

## c1Hints (five rungs; the fifth gives the whole answer)

1. Kind 9's column is the call's column and the jump's column together.
2. A common mistake: leaving BCONST at 0. Then the ALU adds RB, not the constant, and the PC
   takes RA + RB.
3. The arm: `4'h9: begin WRITEY = 1'b1; BCONST = 1'b1; OP1 = 1'b1; CALL = 1'b1; JUMP = 1'b1; end`
4. The first term of ILLEGAL: `(K == 4'h0) | (K[3] & (K != 4'h8) & (K != 4'h9))`.
5. The whole change: the arm in hint 3, the term in hint 4, and the calls' and jumps' term:
   `(((K == 4'h6) | (K == 4'h7) | (K == 4'h9)) & (J != 4'h0))`.

## callFaultsLead (above the fault figure; about 100 words)

1. The figure runs the program that chooses a room, in a machine with the new instruction drawn.
   In its decoder, CALL is an OR of kind 6's line and kind 9's, and JUMP an OR of kind 7's line
   and kind 9's.
2. "orCall made an AND": CALL is kind 6's line AND kind 9's, which is 0 for every kind.
3. "orJump made an AND": JUMP is kind 7's line AND kind 9's, also 0 for every kind.
4. Choose a fault and press "Run until it stops". Before you do, say what you think R15 will
   hold. [Do not say what happens; that is in the outcomes.]

## callFaultsOutcomes (shown after a run; about 150 words, a list allowed)

1. "orCall made an AND": the new instruction is no longer a call.
   - From READ it goes to ALU, then WRITE: 4 edges.
   - Without CALL, register Y takes HR, not PC + 4. R15 takes `00C`, the routine's own address.
   - The PC still takes RA + c, `00C`. showA runs and the display shows -184.
   - showA's jump goes back to R15, `00C`, so showA runs again, for ever. "Run until it stops"
     gives up.
2. "orJump made an AND": the PC no longer takes the ALU's result.
   - The PC takes the call's target, PC + 4c. With c at 0 that is `004`, the instruction itself.
   - R15 takes `008` at every pass, and the call runs again, for ever. The display shows 0.
