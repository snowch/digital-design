# Brief 5C: lesson `new-instruction` (Module 9, lesson 5), explanation to the end

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here. Every Module 9 term is
allowed.

Keys to write: `explanation`, `newColumnLead`, `generalisation`, `newEdgesLead`,
`writeMachineLead`, `c2Task`, `c2Hints.1` to `c2Hints.5`, `reflection`, `modelVsReality`.

## explanation (above the table; about 120 words, a list allowed)

1. The new instruction is one column in the decoder and two changes to its checks.
2. In the drawn decoder, kind 9 has a line, KIND9. Kind 6's and kind 7's lines are KIND6 and
   KIND7, and CALL and JUMP are OR gates: CALL is KIND6 OR KIND9, JUMP is KIND7 OR KIND9.
3. KIND9 also joins the ORs of WRITEY, BCONST and OP1.
4. When CALL and JUMP are both 1, register Y takes PC + 4, and the PC takes the ALU's result. In
   the machine's text, `if (JUMP) NEXT = RESULT;` comes after the line for CALL, so JUMP wins.
5. The controller reads CALL at READ and goes to WRITE, as for a call.

## newColumnLead (above the table of kinds 6, 7 and 9; about 40 words)

1. The table is read off the drawn decoder with the new instruction.
2. Kind 9's column has every 1 that the call's column has, and every 1 that the jump's has.

## generalisation (about 110 words)

1. A new instruction can need four things: a column in the decoder, a place in the checks, its
   states, and new parts in the datapath.
2. This one needed the first two. Its transfers were ones the machine already made, and its
   edges were the call's.
3. An instruction the ALU cannot do needs more. Module 7's ALU does no shifts, so a shift would
   need a new part in the datapath.
4. Kinds A to F stay free in the course's machine. Module 10 asks how to choose what to put
   there.

## newEdgesLead (above the table of kinds 6, 7 and 9; about 40 words)

1. The table is read off the decoder with the new instruction, and the controller.
2. A call and the new instruction take FETCH, READ, WRITE. A jump takes FETCH, READ, ALU. All
   three take 3 edges.

## writeMachineLead (above the challenge; one sentence)

Add kind 9 to the whole machine's text, and run it end to end.

## c2Task (about 150 words, a list allowed)

1. The text is the whole machine: the module `machine`, the controller and the decoder. The
   machine uses the course's modules for the register file, the ALU, the condition, the memory
   and the stop logic.
2. The start is this module's machine, which refuses kind 9 as an illegal instruction.
3. Make the decoder changes of the last challenge in the machine's decoder.
4. The memory holds the program that chooses a room.
5. The tests reset the machine. Then they check the state and the PC after every edge of every
   instruction. During the call a reset rises and falls while CLK is 1. At the stop they check
   HALT and the display, -184. 23 tests.
6. The start fails 17. The first is "004, edge 2 (READ): WRITE after it": it halts at kind 9.

## c2Hints (five rungs; the fifth gives the whole answer)

1. Only the decoder module changes. The module `machine` and the controller already handle a
   word with CALL and JUMP both at 1.
2. A common mistake: sending kind 9 through ALU in the controller. Then it takes 4 edges, and the
   tests expect 3.
3. In the decoder's `case`, after kind 7's arm:
   `4'h9: begin WRITEY = 1'b1; BCONST = 1'b1; OP1 = 1'b1; CALL = 1'b1; JUMP = 1'b1; end`
4. In ILLEGAL, the first term becomes `(K == 4'h0) | (K[3] & (K != 4'h8) & (K != 4'h9))`.
5. The whole change, in the decoder: the arm in hint 3, the term in hint 4, and the calls' and
   jumps' term `(((K == 4'h6) | (K == 4'h7) | (K == 4'h9)) & (J != 4'h0))`.

## reflection (about 110 words)

1. You added an instruction to the machine: a column in the decoder and two changes to its
   checks. The simulator ran it end to end, edge by edge.
2. Two machines now run the same instructions. Module 8's takes one edge for each. This module's
   takes 3 to 5, and keeps one memory port. Both leave the same registers and memory after
   every instruction.
3. Which instructions a machine has is a choice. So is how their words are laid out.
4. End on the question: why are the course's instructions the ones they are, and how should the
   free kinds be used?

## modelVsReality (about 90 words)

1. A chip cannot change once it is made. A new instruction means a new chip.
2. Programs written for the old chip must still run on the new one. So a maker gives new
   instructions kinds the old chip refused, as kind 9 was.
3. Your kind 9 lives only in your copy of the machine's text. The course's machine still
   refuses kind 9, with cause `21`.
