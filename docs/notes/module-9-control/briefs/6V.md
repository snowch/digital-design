# Brief 6V: the words of Module 9's figures, and the blocks' labels

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here, except that a label is short
and need not be a sentence. Every Module 9 term is allowed: these words appear only in Module 9's
figures.

The lessons quote some of these words; those keys are marked FIXED and must come back exactly as
given. Draft the others. Each key is one line. `{name}` in braces is a slot the figure fills: keep
every slot exactly.

## The decoder's table (one column per kind, one row per signal)

- `tableCaption`: the table's caption: the decoder's control signals, kind by kind. At most 8
  words.
- `signal`: the first column's heading, the signal's name. One word.
- `kindHeading`: FIXED "{kind}". `jobBit`: FIXED "J{bit}".
- `kinds.1` to `kinds.9`: under the table, one line per kind: its number and its name. FIXED:
  "1 register job", "2 constant job", "3 load", "4 store", "5 branch", "6 call", "7 jump",
  "8 system job", "9 call through a register".
- `jobBitNote`: under the table: what a cell such as J2 means: the signal is that bit of the job
  digit. One sentence.

## The map of kinds and jobs (one row per kind, one column per job)

- `mapCaption`: the map's caption: which kinds and jobs the decoder takes as instructions. At most
  8 words.
- `mapCorner`: FIXED "K\J". `legal`: FIXED "✓". `illegal`: FIXED "·". `depends`: FIXED "c".
- `cellLabel`: what a screen reader hears for a cell: FIXED "K {k}, J {j}: {meaning}".
- `legalMeaning`: a cell that is an instruction, at most 3 words. `illegalMeaning`: a cell that
  is an illegal instruction, at most 3 words. `dependsMeaning`: an instruction only when the
  constant is 0 to 4, at most 10 words.
- `legend`: under the map: what ✓, · and c mean, in one line.

## Each kind's edges (one row per kind)

- `edgesCaption`: the table's caption: the edges each kind takes. At most 6 words.
- `kind`, `states`, `edges`: the three column headings, one word each: the kind, the states it
  passes through, the number of edges.
- `then`: FIXED " → ".

## The views of one edge

- `opsCaption`: FIXED "The instruction's edges". `edge`, `state`: column headings, one word
  each. `ops`: the column of the edge's register transfers, one word.
- `nextMark`: in the row of the edge still to come, one word.
- `opsNone`: before any edge of the instruction. One short sentence.
- FIXED, all of them, since they are the course's notation for transfers: `opFetch`
  "IR ← memory[PC]"; `opRead` "HA ← R{a}, HB ← R{b}"; `opAlu` "HR ← {left} {job} {right}";
  `opAluCopy` "HR ← {right}"; `opAluUp` "HR ← {left} + 1"; `opAluDown` "HR ← {left} - 1";
  `opLoadWord` "HM ← word[HR]"; `opLoadByte` "HM ← byte[HR]"; `opStoreWord` "word[HR] ← HB";
  `opStoreByte` "byte[HR] ← HB"; `opWrite` "R{y} ← {from}"; `opPc` "PC ← {to}"; `opJoin` "; ";
  `sources` and `jobs` as they are.
- `opNone`: the transfers of an edge at which the machine halts: none, and the machine stops. At
  most 6 words, starting with a lower-case letter.
- `signalsCaption`: FIXED "Control signals at the next edge". `value`: the column of values, one
  word.
- `statesTitle`: the heading over the state diagram: the controller's states. At most 4 words.
- `timingTitle`: the heading over the timing diagram: the edges so far. At most 4 words.

## The blocks' labels, written above each block in the drawings

FIXED, since the lessons name them: `control-unit` "control unit"; `control-decoder` "decoder";
`controller` "controller"; `kind-lines` "kind lines"; `control-signals` "control signals";
`control-signals-call` "control signals"; `decode-checks` "checks"; `kind-check` "kind check";
`job-check` "job check"; `number-check` "number check"; `system-jobs` "system jobs";
`constant-bits` "bits of c"; `cause-word` "cause"; `controller-next` "next-state logic";
`controller-outputs` "output logic"; `word-register-3` "register"; `held-32` "register";
`word-register-32` "register".

Draft, each at most 3 words, lower case:
- `datapath-edges`: the block holding the datapath of this machine.
- `memory-port`: the block holding the memory, reached by one address.
- `state-lines`: the block that turns the state's code into one line per state.
- `held-64`: a register that holds one 64-bit word from one edge to the next (HR, HM).
- `hold-ab`: the block of the two registers HA and HB.
- `split-control`: a block that takes some signals out of the control signals' bus.
- `join-control`: the block that puts the control signals onto one bus.
