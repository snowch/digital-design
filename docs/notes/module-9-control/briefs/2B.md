# Brief 2B: lesson `illegal-instructions` (Module 9, lesson 2), construction and faults

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here. "control unit" and
"illegal instruction" are allowed. "instruction register" and "micro-operation" must not
appear.

Keys to write: `construction`, `writeOutsideLead`, `c1Task`, `c1Hints.1` to `c1Hints.5`,
`checkFaultsLead`, `checkFaultsOutcomes`.

## construction (about 80 words)

1. The check on the number, as text.
2. C is 12 bits, read signed. 0 to 4 are `000` to `004`: bits 11 to 3 are all 0, and bits 2 to 0
   are `000` to `100`.
3. A negative number has bit 11 at 1. So does any number from `800` to `FFF`.
4. So C is outside 0 to 4 when any of bits 11 to 3 is 1, or when bit 2 is 1 with bit 1 or bit 0
   (5, 6 and 7).

## writeOutsideLead (above the challenge; one sentence)

Write OUTSIDE: 1 when C, read signed, is not 0 to 4.

## c1Task (about 90 words)

1. The module `outside` has input C (12 bits) and output OUTSIDE.
2. The start reads only bits 2 to 0: `assign OUTSIDE = C[2] & (C[1] | C[0]);`. It gives 1 for 5,
   6 and 7.
3. The tests try 13 values: 0 to 8, `7FF` (2047), `800` (-2048), `FFC` (-4) and `FFF` (-1).
4. The start fails 3 of them. [Do not say which.]
5. Allowed: `assign`, part selects such as `C[11:3]`, `|`, `&`, `~`, `==` and `!=`.

## c1Hints (five rungs; the fifth gives the whole answer)

1. Try 8, `008`, on the start. Bits 2 to 0 are `000`, so the start gives 0. Which bits of 8 are 1?
2. A common mistake: checking bit 11 alone. That catches the negative numbers but not 8 to 2047.
3. Any of bits 11 to 3 at 1 puts C outside 0 to 4: `(C[11:3] != 9'h0)`.
4. Keep the start's term for 5, 6 and 7, and OR the two.
5. The whole answer: `assign OUTSIDE = (C[11:3] != 9'h0) | (C[2] & (C[1] | C[0]));`

## checkFaultsLead (above the fault lab; about 90 words)

1. The figure shows the decoder's "checks" block. Each fault breaks one check.
2. "NOKIND stuck at 0": the kind check never says that no kind line is 1.
3. "BADNUMBER stuck at 0": the number check never refuses a number.
4. The checks give the decoder seven words: all zeros, kind 9, job 8 of a register job, job 2 of
   kind 8 with 5, job 3 of kind 8 with -1, job 2 of kind 8 with 4, and an add.
5. Choose a fault and press "Run checks". Before you do, say which words you think the fault
   lets through. [Do not say which; that is in the outcomes.]

## checkFaultsOutcomes (shown after a run; about 120 words, a list allowed)

1. "NOKIND stuck at 0": two checks fail. The word of all zeros and kind 9 get CAUSED `00`
   instead of `21`. Every control signal is 0 for them, so the machine would run them as words
   that do nothing and go on to the next. The job check does not catch them: it reads the kind
   lines, and none is 1.
2. "BADNUMBER stuck at 0": two checks fail. Job 2 with 5 and job 3 with -1 get `00` instead of
   `21`. STOP is 1 for both, so the machine stops at them as jobs a later module builds, as
   Module 8's machine did.
3. The other checks pass under both faults: each check covers words the other two do not.
