# Brief 2A: lesson `illegal-instructions` (Module 9, lesson 2), first part

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here. "control unit" is allowed.
This lesson introduces **illegal instruction**: set it in bold where the brief says, plain
meaning first. "instruction register" and "micro-operation" must not appear.

Keys to write: `question`, `motivation`, `prediction`, `p1Question`, `p1Explain`,
`checksOpenLead`, `checksOpenAfter`.

## question (about 80 words)

1. Lesson 1 ended on a question: what should the machine do with a word that is no instruction?
2. Kind 0 and kinds 9 to F have no kind line. A register job has jobs 0 to 7 only, so job 8 is
   no job.
3. The word `82000005` is kind 8, job 2, constant 5. Module 8's machine stops at it, as a system
   job a later module builds.
4. That job copies a control register into register Y, and the constant says which one. The
   machine has five control registers, numbered 0 to 4. Module 12 builds them. There is no
   control register 5.
5. End on the question: how does the decoder tell that a word is no instruction?

## motivation (about 120 words, a list allowed)

1. A word the machine must refuse is an **illegal instruction**. Introduce the term here, plain
   meaning first.
2. A word is illegal for one of three reasons:
   - its kind is 0 or 9 to F;
   - its job is one its kind does not define: jobs 8 to F of kinds 1, 2 and 5; jobs other than
     0, 1, 8 and 9 of a load or a store; any job but 0 of a call or a jump; jobs 5 to F of kind 8;
   - it is job 2 or 3 of kind 8, and its constant, read signed, names no control register: it is
     outside 0 to 4.
3. Kind 8's jobs: 0 stops the machine with cause `41` until Module 12 builds what it does; 1, 2
   and 3 stop it as jobs a later module builds; 4 is `stop`.
4. At an illegal instruction the decoder gives CAUSED `21`, and the machine stops.
5. Module 8's decoder read K and J only. The third reason needs the constant, so Module 9's
   decoder also takes C.

## p1Question (one sentence)

What cause does the decoder give for `84000005`, kind 8, job 4, with constant 5?

## p1Explain (about 70 words)

1. CAUSED is `00`: the word is `stop`, and the machine stops at it with no fault.
2. Only jobs 2 and 3 of kind 8 name a control register. The check on the number counts only for
   them.
3. Job 4 names none, so its constant is not read.
4. `82000005`, job 2 with the same constant, gives `21`. The figure below opens the checks on
   that word.

## checksOpenLead (above the investigation's figure; about 120 words, a list allowed)

1. The figure opens the decoder's "checks" block. The instruction is `82000005`: kind 8, job 2,
   constant 5.
2. Its blocks:
   - "kind check" gives NOKIND, 1 when no kind line is 1.
   - "job check" gives BADJOB, 1 when the job is one its kind does not define.
   - "number check" gives BADNUMBER, 1 when job 2 or 3 of kind 8 names a number outside 0 to 4.
   - An OR gate joins the three into ILLEGAL.
   - "system jobs" gives STOP, 1 for jobs 1 to 4 of kind 8.
   - "cause" gives CAUSED: `21` when ILLEGAL is 1, `41` for job 0 of kind 8, `00` otherwise.
3. Press a block to open it. Press K's, J's and C's pins to try other words.

## checksOpenAfter (below the figure; about 140 words, a list allowed)

1. The checks read the kind lines, not K. "kind check" is one NOR gate of the eight kind lines:
   NOKIND is 1 when none is 1.
2. "job check" has one AND gate per group of kinds:
   - kinds 1, 2 and 5 with J3 (jobs 8 to F);
   - loads and stores with J2 or J1;
   - calls and jumps with any job bit at 1;
   - kind 8 with J3, or with J2 and one of J1 and J0 (jobs 5 to F).
3. "number check" reads C through "bits of c". C, read signed, is outside 0 to 4 when any of
   bits 11 to 3 is 1, or when bit 2 is 1 with bit 1 or bit 0.
4. For `82000005`, C is `005`: bits 2 and 0 are 1. So BADNUMBER is 1, ILLEGAL is 1, and CAUSED
   is `21`.
5. With C at `004`, the word is legal, and STOP is 1.
