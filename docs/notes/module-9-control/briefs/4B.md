# Brief 4B: lesson `micro-operations` (Module 9, lesson 4), construction and faults

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here. "control unit", "illegal
instruction", "instruction register" and "micro-operation" are allowed.

Keys to write: `construction`, `writeOutputsLead`, `c1Task`, `c1Hints.1` to `c1Hints.5`,
`signalFaultsLead`, `signalFaultsOutcomes`.

## construction (about 140 words, a list allowed)

1. Each control signal is the state's line, AND what the kind needs, AND GO.
2. GO is 1 until the stop logic halts the machine. ANDing it in means a halting edge changes no
   register.
3. The signals:
   - FETCHING is FETCH; IREN is FETCH AND GO.
   - CHECKING is READ; HOLDAB is READ AND GO.
   - HOLDR is ALU AND GO.
   - MLOAD is MEMORY AND LOAD; MSTORE is MEMORY AND STORE; HOLDM is MEMORY AND LOAD AND GO.
   - WREG is WRITE AND WRITEY AND GO.
   - PCEN is 1 at the instruction's last edge, AND GO.
4. FETCHING and CHECKING say which checks count, so they do not need GO.
5. MLOAD and MSTORE tell the memory what the edge does. The memory takes GO on a wire of its own.

## c1Task (about 130 words)

1. The module `outputs` has inputs S (the state's 3-bit code), GO, MEM, WRITEY, LOAD and STORE.
   Its outputs are FETCHING, IREN, CHECKING, HOLDAB, HOLDR, MLOAD, MSTORE, HOLDM, WREG and PCEN.
2. The codes: FETCH `000`, READ `001`, ALU `010`, MEMORY `011`, WRITE `100`. A state's line is a
   comparison: `(S == 3'b001)` is READ.
3. The start has FETCHING and IREN. Every other output is 0.
4. PCEN is 1 at an instruction's last edge: WRITE; MEMORY when WRITEY is 0 (a store); ALU when
   MEM and WRITEY are both 0 (a branch or a jump). Then AND GO.
5. The tests try every state with a job, a load, a store and a branch, with GO at 1 and at 0: 40
   tests.

## c1Hints (five rungs; the fifth gives the whole answer)

1. Copy IREN's line for each signal that lets a register take a word, with that signal's state.
2. A common mistake: leaving GO out of WREG. Then a halting edge in WRITE still writes register Y.
3. `assign WREG = (S == 3'b100) & WRITEY & GO;`
4. Name the last edge first: `logic ENDS;` and `assign ENDS = (S == 3'b100) | ((S == 3'b011) & ~WRITEY) | ((S == 3'b010) & ~MEM & ~WRITEY);`. Then `assign PCEN = ENDS & GO;`.
5. The whole answer:

   ```
   logic ENDS;
   assign FETCHING = (S == 3'b000);
   assign IREN = (S == 3'b000) & GO;
   assign CHECKING = (S == 3'b001);
   assign HOLDAB = (S == 3'b001) & GO;
   assign HOLDR = (S == 3'b010) & GO;
   assign MLOAD = (S == 3'b011) & LOAD;
   assign MSTORE = (S == 3'b011) & STORE;
   assign HOLDM = (S == 3'b011) & LOAD & GO;
   assign WREG = (S == 3'b100) & WRITEY & GO;
   assign ENDS = (S == 3'b100) | ((S == 3'b011) & ~WRITEY) | ((S == 3'b010) & ~MEM & ~WRITEY);
   assign PCEN = ENDS & GO;
   ```

## signalFaultsLead (above the fault figure; about 100 words)

1. The figure runs the margin program. With no fault it stops after 18 edges, and the display
   shows 66.
2. "PCEN stuck at 1": the PC takes its next value at every edge.
3. "MSTORE stuck at 0": the memory is never told to store.
4. Choose a fault and press "Run until it stops". Before you do, say what you think the display
   will show. The view of the instruction's edges shows each edge's micro-operations. [Do not
   say what happens; that is in the outcomes.]

## signalFaultsOutcomes (shown after a run; about 140 words, a list allowed)

1. "PCEN stuck at 1": the PC moves on at every edge, not once per instruction.
   - Edge 1 fetches R1 ← -184 at `000`, and the PC moves to `004`.
   - Edges 2, 3 and 4 finish R1 ← -184, while the PC moves to `008`, `00C` and `010`.
   - Edge 5 fetches the word at `010`: `stop`.
   - The machine stops after 6 edges. R1 is -184. R2 and R3 are never written, and the display
     shows 0.
2. "MSTORE stuck at 0": every edge runs as it should, and the machine stops after 18 edges. R3
   is 66. At the store's MEMORY edge the only micro-operation is PC ← PC + 4. The display shows
   0.
