Keys to write: `question`, `motivation`, `prediction`, `p1Question`, `p1Explain`,

## question (about 60 words)

1. Lesson 3 ended on a question: which control signals make each edge do its work, and how does
   the controller set them?
2. An instruction such as R3 ← R1 - R2 is one register transfer in Module 8's machine. Here it
   takes 4 edges.
3. End on the question: what does each of those edges do, and which signals make it?

## motivation (about 130 words, a list allowed)

1. Each edge of an instruction makes one or two register transfers of its own. One such transfer
   is a **micro-operation**. Introduce the term here, plain meaning first.
2. R3 ← R1 - R2 is four edges of micro-operations:
   - FETCH: IR ← memory[PC];
   - READ: HA ← R1, HB ← R2;
   - ALU: HR ← HA - HB;
   - WRITE: R3 ← HR, and PC ← PC + 4.
3. A register takes a word at an edge only when its enable is 1: IREN for IR, HOLDAB for HA and
   HB, HOLDR for HR, HOLDM for HM, WREG for register Y, PCEN for the PC.
4. The controller sets those enables from its state. Lesson 3 built its next-state logic; this
   lesson builds its output logic.

## p1Question (one or two sentences)

Two edges of R1 ← -184 have passed. Which register takes a word at the next edge?

## p1Explain (about 80 words)

1. HR takes a word: HR ← c, the constant -184.
2. The next edge is the instruction's third, in the state ALU. The controller sets HOLDR to 1
   there, and only there.
3. IR took the instruction at the first edge, in FETCH.
4. R1 is written at the fourth edge, in WRITE, from HR. At the same edge the PC moves on to
   `004`.

## fourViewsAfter (below the figure; about 130 words, a list allowed)

1. At the load's ALU edge, AZERO and BCONST are 1. The micro-operation is HR ← 0 + c: the
   address `7D8`.
2. At its MEMORY edge, HOLDM is 1: HM ← word[HR].
3. At its WRITE edge, WREG and PCEN are both 1: R2 ← HM, and PC ← PC + 4.
4. At the branch's ALU edge, `008`, the micro-operations are HR ← HA - HB and PC ← PC + 4. The
   branch is not taken: -184 is not less than -250. HOLDR is 1, though nothing uses HR after.
5. Every signal that lets a register take a word is 1 only in its own state.
