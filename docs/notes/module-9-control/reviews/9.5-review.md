## Review: 9.5 new-instruction (read as a learner who has done 6.1 to 9.4)

I checked these against the files and found them correct: the word `9040F000` and the field layout (docs/isa.md); the 3-edge answer; the 21 edges, -184 on the display and R15 = `008`; both fault outcomes; the 263 and 23 test counts and each start failing 17 (all pinned in `content/lessons/new-instruction.facts.test.ts`); "word for Y" (`packages/dd-views/src/parts.ts:152`); and the KIND6, KIND7 and KIND9 lines and the orCall and orJump gates (`packages/dd-model/src/control.ts:81-86, 178-193`). The drawn next PC also lets JUMP win, because pickJump comes last (`datapath.ts:1402`), so the prose's claim about the text holds for the drawing as well.

### Findings, worst first

**1. The lines a learner knows as CALL and JUMP are renamed without a word (failure experiment, then explanation).** Lesson 9.1 taught: "Kind 3's line is LOAD … kind 6's CALL, kind 7's JUMP." This lesson's fault lead says "CALL is an OR of kind 6's line and kind 9's line". Read with 9.1, that is "CALL is an OR of CALL and kind 9's line". The renaming ("Kind 6's line is KIND6") only arrives in the explanation, after the faults, and no sentence says why the name moved: CALL and JUMP stopped being bare kind lines and became new OR gates. The fault labels "orCall" and "orJump" name gates the learner has never seen, because 9.1 had no orCall. This also cuts against 9.1's generalisation ("Adding a kind adds one line and joins it to the OR of every signal that kind needs"). Here two signals that had no OR gate gain one. Direction: say before the fault lab, and in the construction, that two new OR gates appear and that the kind lines lose the names CALL and JUMP.

**2. The interactive does not show a choice made at run time (investigation, "choose").** The motivation says "the program can choose the place while it runs", and the lead says "The office chooses at run time which room's reading the display shows." But the program's first instruction is `R4 ← 00C`, a constant written into the program. Nothing chosen while the program runs decides R4. The only alternative is the sentence "With R4 ← `018` instead…", which again means editing the program. A learner can fairly ask how this differs from a plain call with a different constant. Direction: either let something the program reads decide R4, or soften the claim to what the figure shows.

**3. "Kinds A to F stay free in the course's machine" (generalisation) contradicts the page and docs/isa.md.** The Model versus reality section says "The course's machine still refuses kind 9", and docs/isa.md line 255 says "Kinds 9 to F are free". Kind 9 is taken only in the learner's copy. Direction: make clear which machine the sentence means.

**4. The reflection's summary does not match the earlier lessons.** "This module's takes 3 to 5": lesson 9.3's figure text says "The stop takes 2 edges". "Two machines now run the same instructions" comes straight after a lesson in which the learner's machine gained kind 9 and Module 8's did not. "Both leave the same registers and memory after every instruction" is asserted, not shown on this page. Direction: check the range and qualify the "same instructions" claim.

**5. "It needs no ALU edge. The ALU is gates." (text shown after the prediction) gives the wrong reason.** The ALU is gates for a jump too, yet a jump passes through ALU. The real reason is the next sentence's: the decoder sets CALL, and the controller sends a call from READ to WRITE. The following line, "A jump also takes the PC from the ALU at the first edge after READ…", partly repairs this, but the learner meets the wrong cause first. Direction: lead with CALL routing the controller. Use "the ALU's result is ready" as support, not as the cause.

**6. The same point is made three or four times.** The explanation's "A new instruction is one column in the decoder and two changes to its checks"; the generalisation's "This one needed only the first two"; the reflection's "a column in the decoder and two changes to its checks". The construction's three bullets already say it. The question section also asks its question twice: "Which parts of the machine would have to change?" and then "How much of the machine must change for one new instruction?" The new-edges lead repeats the prediction's FETCH, READ, WRITE as well. Direction: keep each point once, where it is earned.

**7. Machine-text, hint 2: "A common mistake: sending kind 9 through ALU in the controller."** The controller never reads the kind. It reads CALL, MEM and WRITEY, and hint 1 says the controller needs no change. A learner cannot route kind 9 anywhere in the controller. The mistake that produces 4 edges is leaving CALL at 0 in the decoder's arm. Direction: describe the mistake in terms of the signal the learner sets.

**8. Machine-text task: "During the call a reset rises and falls while CLK is 1."** No reason is given. A learner may think they must write something for it. Direction: say in a few words what the test checks: that a reset acts only at an edge, so the decoder change must not disturb that. A second point I cannot judge from the text: the task says "The challenge has 23 tests" while the page lists 46 labelled steps, half of them "clock low". If the UI shows the 46 labels, the learner sees two numbers. Please check how the panel counts them.

**9. Decoder-text task: "kind 9 with constant `008`".** Nothing says why this one case uses a non-zero constant (presumably so that a missing BCONST shows, as hint 2 describes). Low cost, but a learner will wonder. Direction: tie it to what it catches, without giving the arm away.

**10. Smaller points.**
- Explanation: "In the machine's text, `if (JUMP) NEXT = RESULT;` comes after the line for CALL". The learner has not yet seen this lesson's machine text; it appears only in the closing challenge. Lesson 8.5's drawn "next PC" block, which the learner knows, would ground the point. Mixing "drawn decoder" with "machine's text" in one paragraph is a small shift of source.
- Motivation: "Kind 9 is the first kind the machine leaves free." Kind 0 is refused too. That is fine given docs/isa.md, but "free" is used here without the learner having been told that kinds 9 to F are reserved for additions. Lesson 9.2 only said they are illegal.
- Generalisation: "A new instruction can need four things: …, its states, …" "states" means the controller's states, but the same page uses "state" for machine state ("the state and the PC after every edge" in the machine-text task). That is one word in two senses on one page.

### What works

The numbers are all produced by the simulator and pinned by a test. The prediction, the run and the two faults each show a distinct consequence: one fault takes 4 edges and loops through showA, the other loops on the call itself. The fault outcomes explain *why* the machine loops, from register contents the learner can see. The construction's "datapath needs no new parts / controller needs no change / decoder is the only place" is a clear, checkable claim that both challenges then test.
