## Review of 9.3 several-edges (read as a learner who has done 6.1 to 9.2)

**What works.** Every number I checked holds. The program's 23 edges are 5+5+3+4+4+2. The margin variant takes 18 edges, the FETCHING fault gives cause `33` after 16, and the row fault ends after 17. Row 4 is ALU→MEMORY when MEM is 1. All of these are pinned in `content/lessons/several-edges.facts.test.ts` and `controllerMachine()` (`packages/dd-model/src/control.ts`). The FETCHING-stuck fault is well chosen: the ADDR bus shows `00C` where `7C0` belongs. Both challenges' tasks, hints and tests match. The test counts (8 and 44) count only the steps that check something; the label lists also include steps with no check.

### Findings, ranked by how much they would hurt a learner

**1. The failure experiment uses a table the learner has not seen yet** (edge-faults lead and outcome)
- The lead says "Row 4 stuck at 0" "breaks row 4 of the controller's table". That table is first shown in the next section, in the controller figure.
- The outcome says "No other row applies, so the controller transitions to FETCH instead." It does not say why no matching row means FETCH: FETCH's code is `000`, and no row's AND gate drives a 1.
- The figure's settings have no `states` and no `timing`, and the buses are only IR and ADDR. The learner cannot see the controller skip MEMORY. They see only 17 edges and a display of 0.
- Direction: either move the fault after the controller figure, or describe the row in words here. Also give this figure the states or timing view, so the skipped state can be seen.

**2. IR changes from a bus to a register, and the lesson never says so** (motivation, prediction lead)
- 8.3 told the learner: "IR is a bus: the ROM's output at PC. It is not a register."
- This lesson introduces "the **instruction register**" but never says it is the IR they know, now made a register. The learner has to work out that IR stands for it from "IREN (IR)" and from the challenge.
- The prediction lead then calls IR and HR "the buses IR and HR", although both are now registers.
- Direction: set the new IR against Module 8's in one or two sentences (style rule 10). Make clear that the "buses" table shows the registers' outputs.

**3. A wrong cause for a branch** (generalisation)
- "The ALU's result decides the next PC for a branch". In 8.5 the flags decide, through the condition block's MET; the result decides only for a jump.
- Direction: name the flags, or MET, for the branch.

**4. PCEN is not 1 once per instruction** (colder-edges "After")
- "PCEN is 1 once per instruction, at its last edge." In the model PCEN is ENDS AND GO, and GO is 0 at the stop's halting edge.
- The lesson itself says "a stop keeps the PC on the stop". So the diagram shows 5 PCEN pulses for 6 instructions.
- Direction: except the stop, or say why it has no pulse.

**5. "and no more" clashes with the prediction's own trace** (generalisation, prediction outcome)
- The generalisation says "Each kind takes as many edges as it has work for, and no more."
- The prediction outcome says of the load's READ edge: "The load uses neither".
- A call (3 edges: FETCH, READ, WRITE) reads no registers either.
- A learner will ask why these kinds pass through READ at all. The answer is spread across the explanation: the decoder's checks run only in READ, and READ reads CALL to choose the next state.
- Direction: say plainly why every kind passes through READ, or soften the "no more" claim.

**6. The same reason given for different edge counts** (colder-edges "After")
- "The branch takes 3: it writes no register." and "The store takes 4: it writes no register." The same reason gives 3 for one and 4 for the other.
- The missing half of each reason is that the branch uses no memory edge and the store does. The learner is left to work that out (rule 9).
- Direction: give the complete reason for each count.

**7. An incomplete list of what an edge does** (generalisation)
- "Each handles one register read, one ALU job, or one memory access." This leaves out the WRITE edge's register write.
- READ also reads two registers, not one.
- Direction: make the list match the five states.

**8. A loose cross-reference** (edge-faults lead)
- "the margin program from Module 8: it calculates R1 - R2 and stores the result to the display."
- 8.3's margin program ends `R4 ← R3 + R3` and stores nothing. 8.4's program reads the sensors, stores to `400` and lights the lamps.
- Direction: call it a shortened form of Module 8's margin program, or list its instructions as 8.3 did.

**9. A new name for a known part** (generalisation)
- "comes from the next-PC adder". 8.5 calls this part the "next PC" block, with TARGET.
- Direction: use 8.5's name.

**10. Repeats** (rule 16)
- Each kind's edge count is stated in three places: colder-edges "After", the generalisation prose and the kind-edges "After". The controller "After" says much of the same again.
- "A branch and jump end at the ALU edge" appears twice in effect.
- The PC holding the instruction's address appears in both the motivation and the explanation.
- Direction: state the counts once, beside the table that computes them, and let the other places point to it.

**11. One overloaded sentence** (colder-edges lead)
- "Beside the drawing: the controller's states, …; the edges so far as a timing diagram, with one line per signal …: IREN (IR), HOLDAB (HA and HB), …; the buses …, and the display." This is one sentence holding three lists (rules 1 and 8).
- Direction: break it into bullets.

**12. The prediction's answer can be read off the motivation** (prediction)
- The motivation names the five states, with MEMORY and WRITE (register Y) among them. A learner who knows a load reads memory and writes Y can count 5 without predicting anything.
- Direction: consider holding the list of states back until after the prediction, or ask something the list does not answer.

**13. Minor points**
- Fetchport hint 2 calls leaving IR as an `assign` "a common mistake". That is the starting text, so the hint describes not starting rather than a mistake. A mistake learners do make would help more, for example a reset placed outside the clock edge.
- The explanation says "The control unit now has four parts: the digits block, the decoder, the controller and the stop logic". 9.1 said the control unit was "the decoder alone", and nothing says when the digits block and the stop logic joined it.
- "transitions to" is formal next to "goes to", which the rest of the page uses.

I could not check the rendered figures (the label overlap, which rows of the timing diagram are visible). This review rests on the text rendering and the model code.
