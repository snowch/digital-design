Verdicts on the 9.1 control-signals review. I checked them against `packages/dd-model/src/control.ts`, `FaultLab.tsx`, `content/lessons/control-signals*.ts`, `module3-facts.ts`, and the lesson texts for 8.3, 9.2 and state-machines. I changed no files.

**1. Construction gives the first challenge away: Stands.** Construction says "WRITEY is 1 for … 1, 2, 3 and 6. BCONST is 1 for … 2, 3, 4 and 7." That makes the challenge's own hints pointless: hint 1 asks the learner to "Name the kinds that write register Y", and hint 2 warns about the jump. Smallest fix: drop the two kind lists from construction and keep the description of what each signal means. Finding 9's first repeat then goes away too.

**2. "checks" means two things: Stands.** The block is named "checks" (`b.scope("checks", …)`), and so is the test run: "Run checks" and "The checks give seven instructions". One outcomes paragraph uses both: "Two checks fail on OP0" and "the checks treat it as an unknown instruction". "Run checks" is a shared view string (`strings.fault.run`), so change the lesson prose instead: call the block "the checks block" every time, or call the test runs "the seven instructions" or "the runs".

**3. "decoder" means two things: Partly.** Almost every mention of a small decoder already says "Module 3's" or "2-to-4". The one unqualified use is "line 1 of the first decoder AND line 1 of the second". Qualify that one, for example "the 2-to-4 decoder on K3 K2". The rest falls.

**4. The recipe for adding a kind is incomplete: Stands.** `kindCheck` is a NOR over `knownKinds`. A new kind line not added to it leaves NOKIND at 1, so ILLEGAL is 1 and CAUSED is 21. `jobCheck` also lists the kinds explicitly. 9.5 says "one column in the decoder and two changes to its checks". Fix: limit the claim to the control signals and add that the checks must learn the new kind too (9.2 opens them).

**5. The model-versus-reality note contradicts the circuit: Stands, minus one point.** In `signalGates`, JOBS feeds orWritey, andOp2, andOp1 and andOp0, and MEM feeds orBconst, andAzero, andByte and orOp1. So the course already shares terms. "One OR per signal" is also false: AZERO, BYTE and OP2 are ANDs, and LOAD, STORE, BRANCH, CALL and JUMP have no gate. The point that "terms" is never explained falls: fewer-gates already uses "term". Fix: say what a tool does beyond the course's drawing (merges and minimises further, across signals), without claiming the course shares nothing.

**6. The explanation gives the gates the wrong roles: Stands.** The AND per kind (`kind${v}`) builds the kind lines. In state-machines, each row's AND is "the state's line AND the row's inputs". The match for that here is kind line AND job bit, not the AND per kind. Fix: pair each group of gates with its job, as the investigation's bullets do.

**7. The reflection asks what the learner already knows: Partly.** 8.3 says kind 0 gives cause 21 and the machine stops ("illegal instruction"). The undefined-job case was not taught as clearly, so that half is still open. 9.2 opens by quoting this question word for word ("Lesson 1 ended with a question: …"), so any rewording has to change 9.2's opening too. Direction: ask how the decoder's gates could tell that a word is no instruction.

**8. The table lead omits J3: Stands.** The facts test pins AZERO as `J3` for kinds 3 and 4. Add J3 to "A cell is 1, 0, or J2, J1 or J0".

**9. Repeats: Partly.**
- The WRITEY and BCONST lists: real, and they go with fix 1.
- Kinds 0 and 9 to F: the generalisation and reflection repeat each other. Cut one. The challenge task's line falls, because a task must stand on its own.
- The one-edge argument: partly real. The generalisation uses it to contrast with machines that take several edges. Keep that contrast and drop the restated premise.

**10. "each other signal is an OR of the kind lines": Stands.** AZERO, BYTE and OP2 are single ANDs, OP1's OR takes JOB1, OP0's takes JOB0, and WRITEY's takes JOBS, which is not a kind line. Fix: qualify the sentence, for example "an OR of kind lines, or an AND with a job bit". Do not let the next bullet undo it.

**11. C appears before it is explained: Stands.** The opening quotes Module 8, "from K and J alone", and the prediction lead then says "Its inputs are K, J and C" with no reason. Fix: add the investigation's clause there ("C, the constant, which only its checks read"), or leave C out of the prediction lead.

**12. The fault lab's cause comes from outside the drawing: Falls, with one optional tweak.** `FaultLab` compares every output of the `decoder` circuit, whatever the scope, so the learner's failure list does show CAUSED. That answers the reviewer's open question. The page already says where 21 comes from: "the checks treat it as an unknown instruction". LOAD is drawn as an input pin of the signals block, so the stuck line is visible. Optional: "breaks one part of it" could become "breaks one gate or one line into it".

**13. Small points:**
- "`assign` is not an operator": Stands. Say "Allowed: `assign` and the operators …".
- The unexplained `default:`: Falls. `default` was met in 7.3 and 8.5, and the task already says "Kind 8 needs no arm: every signal stays 0."
- "Lesson 3 adds them": Stands. Name it as this module's third lesson.
- "one line goes high for each kind": Stands, but minor. The investigation's "Each line is 1 only for its kind" is the clearer wording; use it here.
- "path from the fetch to the register write": Stands. Stores and branches write no register, so end the path at the edge.

**Where to start:** findings 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 and 11 hold in full. 3, 7 and 9 hold in part. 12 falls, and so does the `default:` point in 13.
