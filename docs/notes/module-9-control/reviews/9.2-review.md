Review of 9.2 illegal-instructions, read as a learner who has done Modules 6 to 9.1. Findings are ranked by how much they would hurt a learner. I checked the numbers against `content/lessons/illegal-instructions.ts`, `illegal-instructions.facts.test.ts` and `packages/dd-model/src/control.ts`.

**1. The `checks-text` tests do not catch the mistake that hint 2 warns about.** Hint 2 says: "do not OR OUTSIDE in on its own, or `stop` with constant 5 will be refused." But the tests (`CHECK_VECTORS`) use constant 0 for every kind and job. They change C only for kind 8, jobs 2 and 3. So an answer that ORs OUTSIDE in with no gating passes all 280 tests, and so does one gated on kind 8 alone. The challenge cannot tell a learner who makes the named mistake that it is wrong. Direction: add vectors that give a non-zero constant to a job that ignores it, for example kind 8 job 4 with 5, or a register job with 5. Then update the stated counts (280 tests, the start fails 105).

**2. "control register" and "system job" are never explained, and `41` is never said to mean anything.** In the Question, "That job copies a control register into register Y, and the constant says which one" uses a term no earlier lesson uses. Module 8's lessons never mention system jobs apart from `stop`; I grepped 8.1 to 8.5. So "Module 8's machine stops at it, as a system job a later module builds" describes something the learner never saw. In the Motivation, "Kind 8: job 0 gives `41`" does not say what `41` is, or whether the machine stops at job 0. The investigation repeats `41` without explaining it. Direction: one plain sentence saying what a control register is (or that Module 12 explains it), what kind 8's jobs are, and what cause `41` means for this machine.

**3. Wrong fact in the Construction.** "This catches negative numbers and values `800` to `FFF`." The values `800` to `FFF` *are* the negative numbers. The sentence leaves out the case bits 11 to 3 actually add, which is 8 to `7FF`. Outside-text hint 2 has it right ("not 8 to 2047"). Direction: name the two ranges correctly. The trailing spaces on lines 88 and 90 should go too.

**4. "check" means two things on this page.** "kind check", "job check", "number check" and "the checks" are parts of the decoder. In the fault lab, "Run checks", "Two checks fail", "The checks test seven instructions" and "The other checks pass" are the test words. It gets worst at "The other checks pass under both faults. Each covers words the other two do not." The first "checks" means test words. "Each … the other two" seems to mean the decoder's three checks. But the lab has only two faults, and none on the job check, so the figure does not show that claim. Direction: give the test words their own name in the prose, and either show the third claim or drop it.

**5. The Explanation says the gates "settle at the same edge."** In "These checks are gates, like the control signals, and settle at the same edge", gates do not settle at an edge. 9.1 says correctly that the signals "settle before the next edge". Direction: say they settle between edges, alongside the control signals.

**6. The fault lab's question does not fit one of its faults.** The lead asks the learner to "predict which instructions the fault lets through." Under BADNUMBER stuck at 0 nothing gets through: "STOP is 1, so the machine stops." Only the cause is wrong. Also, "the machine runs them as do-nothing instructions and continues" (NOKIND) is something the figure, which is the checks block alone, cannot show. Direction: ask what cause each word now gets. Mark "continues" as following from STOP being 0, not as something the figure shows.

**7. The Generalisation claims more than the checks do.** "a jump to a wrong address runs whatever word sits there. The three checks catch it" is not true in general. A stray data word that happens to decode as a legal instruction runs. Direction: say the checks catch such a word only when it is illegal.

Also in the Generalisation, "the job check learns which of its jobs exist" suggests the gate adapts by itself. A designer must add a term.

**8. The prediction is answered before it is asked.** The Motivation lists the third reason as "job 2 or 3 of kind 8" and says "job 4 is `stop`". The prediction on `84000005` then only asks the learner to look that up. Direction: keep the job-2-and-3 restriction until after the prediction, so the trap (a constant of 5 that does not matter) is still a trap.

**9. The same points are made over and over.** The three reasons appear in the Motivation, in the investigation lead (six bullets), in the investigation "After", in the Explanation's first paragraph and again in the Generalisation. `82000005` is described as "kind 8, job 2, constant 5" four times: Question, prediction outcome, investigation lead, "After". "The check on the number counts only for those jobs" and "its constant is not read" say the same thing twice in a row. The Explanation's paragraph adds almost nothing new except "changes no register and no memory word". Direction: cut each to its first full statement.

**10. Smaller points:**

- **"decoder line" and "kind line".** The kind-map's "After" and the Generalisation say "decoder line". 9.1 and the rest of this lesson say "kind line". Pick one.
- **The definition does not fit the main example.** The Motivation defines an illegal instruction as "a word the decoder does not know". But the decoder does know kind 8 job 2. `82000005` is refused because of its constant.
- **The investigation lead lists "system jobs" as outputting only STOP.** The block also outputs SYSTEM, which "cause" reads (`control.ts` `systemJobs`). The drawing will show a wire the lead never names.
- **"bits of c".** Inside "number check", the block label (`parts.ts`) uses a lower-case c, while the prose uses C.
- **The kind-map lead tries only two constants.** It says the figure "tests both constant 0 and constant 5", then says a c marks a word that is legal "when the constant is 0 to 4". Two values cannot show the range. Say the range comes from the number check.
- **Some operators are called gates.** The `outside-text` task calls `==` and `!=` "gates".
- **A wording slip in the Motivation.** "jobs 1 to 3 stop it" should say what "it" is (the machine).

**Checked and correct:**

- the map's counts: 37 legal, 2 c, 217 illegal (39 instructions in all);
- 13 tests and 3 start failures for `outside-text`;
- 280 tests and 105 start failures for `checks-text` (256 + 2 × 12; 89 + 16);
- the investigation's gate descriptions match `control.ts`;
- `82000005` gives CAUSED `21`, and with constant `004` it gives `00` and STOP 1;
- the fault outcomes match the facts test;
- the Reflection's lead-in matches 9.3's opening question.

**What works:** the lesson builds the number check from the bits up, and the construction challenge leads straight into the whole-check challenge. The prediction picks a good trap: `stop` with a constant of 5. The NOKIND fault outcome explains clearly why the job check cannot cover a missing kind, because it reads kind lines that are all 0.
