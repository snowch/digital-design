Sceptic verdicts on the 9.4 (micro-operations) review. I wrote and changed no files. I checked the claims against the lesson text, `content/lessons/micro-operations.prose.ts`, `micro-operations.ts` and `micro-operations.facts.test.ts`, `packages/dd-views/src/interactives/DatapathFigure.tsx` and `ControlViews.tsx`, `packages/lesson-schema/src/validate.ts`, and lessons 8.1 to 9.5 in the scratchpad.

**1. Stands.** The page defines a micro-operation as "One such transfer". It then says "R3 ← R1 - R2 … becomes four micro-operations" and lists 6 transfers over 4 edges. Fix: say "four edges" (or count the 6 transfers), and use one meaning from then on.

**2. Stands.** "Every control signal that makes a register write is 1 only in its own state" is false for PCEN. The same After text has PCEN at 1 in WRITE and the PC moving at the branch's ALU edge. The construction defines PCEN by "the instruction's last edge". Fix: limit the claim to IREN, HOLDAB, HOLDR, HOLDM and WREG, and name PCEN as the exception.

**3. Partly.** What is real:
- The figure's `signals` list has no AZERO or BCONST, yet the After text cites them.
- "the value of every control signal" overclaims. `CONTROL_SIGNALS` in `dd-model/src/control.ts` also holds WRITEY, LOAD, STORE, BYTE, AZERO, BCONST and OP2 to OP0, none of which the table lists.
- No `buses` are set, and the drawing does not write wide buses (DatapathFigure's comment and schema say so). So `7D8` as HR's value appears nowhere as a value.

What falls: the micro-operations view shows the operations symbolically. `opAlu` renders "HR ← 0 + c" and `opLoadWord` renders "HM ← word[HR]". So "the ALU does 0 + c" and "HM reads the word" are on screen.

Fix: add AZERO and BCONST to `signals` and HR to `buses` (or drop those names from the After text), and narrow "every control signal" to "the controller's signals".

**4. Stands, on accuracy.** The construction line comes straight before a list that qualifies it, and its paragraph explains FETCHING, CHECKING, MLOAD and MSTORE. The generalisation ("Each control signal is built the same way: the state's line, AND what the kind needs, AND GO") and the reflection ("Each control signal is the two together, AND GO") are flatly false for those four signals, and for IREN, HOLDAB, HOLDR (no kind) and PCEN. Controller-text hint 2 warns against the very mistake the summary invites. Fix: state the rule for the signals that make a register take a word, and keep the exceptions in sight. Cutting the repeat is secondary.

**5. Partly.** The page never says outright that GO can fall between edges. It does give the effect: "GO is ANDed into every signal that makes a register take a word; halting edges then change no registers." The CHECKING exception is covered by "FETCHING and CHECKING mark which checks run, so they do not need GO". 9.3's challenge already tested GO falling while CLK is 1. GO falls as part of READ's settle, not as an outside input, so the explanation's settle paragraph is not wrong. Fix: one sentence in the explanation saying GO can fall during READ's settle, and that the GO-ANDed enables fall with it before the edge. Low priority.

**6. Partly.** It is true that controller-text's outputs are outputs-text's answer with the state names put in, and that the task text says "For PCEN, compare `next` with FETCH". But the explanation already gives that idea ("PCEN uses the next-state logic: it is 1 when the next state is FETCH"). Joining two parts into one module is a legitimate closing challenge, and nothing in `docs/authoring.md` or the style checklist forbids it. Fix: drop the PCEN instruction from the task text so the learner connects the explanation to `next`. Redesigning the challenge is not needed.

**7. Stands, minor.** "Every edge runs as it should" contradicts "the only micro-operation is PC ← PC + 4" at the store's MEMORY edge. Fix: say the states and the 18 edges are unchanged.

**8. Stands.** "needs no new hardware" is followed by "a column of signals in the decoder", which is gates. 9.5's originality note confirms the right line: "It needs no datapath part … the work is control alone". Fix: say the datapath needs no change, and leave the rest to 9.5.

**9. Stands, minor.** 8.3's margin program ends "R4 ← R3 + R3", "stop", and calls R2 "the limit". 8.4's "show-margin" reads the sensors and stores to RAM. Neither is this program, which stores R3 to the display. The same wording is in 9.3's edge-faults lead, so fix both: "a version of Module 8's margin program that shows R3 on the display".

**10. Smaller points:**
- **"a constant job whose job is copy B": Partly.** "Constant job" is the kind's name (8.2), so "job" appears in two senses within four words. Fix: follow 8.1's form, "R4 ← R2, job 5, copy B", for example "a constant job, job 5 (copy B)".
- **"A register writes a word": Stands.** It reverses the direction. The rest of the page says "takes a word" or "is written". Fix: "takes a word".
- **"four linked views" plus a state diagram and a timing diagram: Falls.** The lead names the four views and then introduces the two diagrams separately ("The state diagram shows … The timing diagram shows …"). The generalisation's "four views" matches the lead's four. Nothing contradicts.
- **Explanation paragraph: Stands.** It breaks style rule 2: it covers why the signals are steady, settling and the edge, and PCEN, all in one paragraph. The "same signal" point is real too. outputs-text built PCEN from ENDS on S, MEM and WRITEY. Against the starting next-state logic, ENDS is exactly `next == FETCH`: WRITE always; MEMORY when WRITEY is 0; ALU when MEM and WRITEY are both 0. The page never says the two are the same signal. Fix: give PCEN its own paragraph, saying the two forms agree.
- **"routine": Stands.** No earlier lesson (6.1 to 9.3) uses the word; it first appears here and then in 9.5. Fix: use words already taught (8.5's "call" and its target address).
- **35 vs 38 tests: Falls.** `testCount` counts only the steps that have `expect`, which is 35 (`validate.ts`, `sim/src/vectors.ts`). The runner's total is built the same way. "38 labelled" belongs to the text dump, which lists every step. 9.3 has the same shape (44 stated, 46 labelled). The page's "35 tests" matches the verdict.

**Priority for fixes:** 1, 2, 4 and 8 are factual contradictions a learner meets head-on. Then 3, 7, 9, and the explanation-paragraph and "routine" items. 5 and 6 are small.
