Review of 9.1 control-signals, read as a learner who has finished Modules 6 to 8. I changed no repository files; the only probe script I wrote is in the scratchpad.

**What works.** The facts I checked all hold:
- The Module 8 quotation in the opening matches 8.5's reflection word for word.
- "Kind 5 … is line 1 of the first decoder AND line 1 of the second" matches `kindLines` in control.ts.
- WRITEY, JOBS, MEM and OP2 are built as the prose says (`signalGates`).
- Both fault outcomes are right. I ran "LOAD stuck at 0" on a load through the decoder circuit: WRITEY, LOAD, BCONST and OP1 go to 0 and CAUSED becomes 21. That is five outputs, as stated.
- The test counts are right: 16, and 39 (24 + 8 + 2 + 5).

The lesson moves cleanly from a closed block to an opened one, then a table, then text.

## Findings, worst first

1. **Construction: the prose gives away the first challenge.** "WRITEY is 1 for kinds that write register Y: 1, 2, 3 and 6. BCONST is 1 for … kinds 2, 3, 4 and 7. … `(K == 4'h3)` is 1 only for kind 3. An OR of the relevant kind lines gives the signal." Together with the kind lists, that is the answer. The learner only has to type it, and the challenge tests nothing. Direction: in construction, leave out which kinds need each signal and let the learner work it out, from the opened decoder or the motivation's descriptions.

2. **"checks" means two different things on one page.** The decoder's block is "checks", which "works out STOP and CAUSED". The fault lab's test instructions are also "the checks", with a "Run checks" button: "The checks give seven instructions". Then the LOAD outcome says "the checks treat it as an unknown instruction", which means the block. A learner cannot tell which is meant. Direction: give the fault lab's test runs a different word in this lesson's prose.

3. **"decoder" means two different things.** The lesson's subject is "the decoder", the instruction decoder. The same page also says "two of Module 3's 2-to-4 decoders", "line 1 of the first decoder" and "Module 3's decoders run on K". Direction: always qualify the small ones ("2-to-4"), or name them by what they read, so that "the decoder" means one thing only.

4. **Generalisation: adding a kind is described incompletely.** "Adding a kind adds one line and joins it to the OR of every signal that kind needs. No other kind's signals change." In the circuit, a new kind's line must also go to the checks (`kindCheck`'s NOR, `jobCheck`). Without that, it gives cause 21. Lesson 9.5 says so: "a column in the decoder and two changes to its checks". As written, this lesson teaches a recipe that fails. Direction: add the checks to the statement, or limit the claim to the control signals.

5. **Model versus reality contradicts the lesson's own circuit.** "Real design tools … share terms between signals… The course keeps one OR per signal". The course's decoder already shares terms: JOBS feeds WRITEY, OP2, OP1 and OP0, and MEM feeds BCONST, OP1, AZERO and BYTE. The investigation shows both. "terms" is also never explained. Direction: say what the course's version leaves out compared with a tool's, without claiming it shares nothing.

6. **Explanation: the gates are given the wrong roles.** "An AND gate per kind and an OR gate per signal build the control signals from the kind lines." The AND gate per kind builds the kind lines themselves. The ANDs that build signals join a line with a job bit. The Module 5 comparison ("AND gates for each row") then pairs the wrong gates. Direction: match each gate group to its job, as the investigation already does correctly.

7. **The reflection asks a question the learner can already answer.** "What should the machine do with a word that is no instruction…?" Module 8.3 taught that kind 0 stops with cause 21. This lesson's own LOAD outcome says "The cause shows `21`… the checks treat it as an unknown instruction". Direction: ask what lesson 9.2 actually opens, for example how the gates detect it, or a job its kind does not define, rather than what the machine should do.

8. **The table's lead lists too few cell values.** "A cell is 1, 0, or J2, J1 or J0". The table also shows J3: AZERO is J3 for kinds 3 and 4 (pinned in control-signals.facts.test.ts), and the "After" text says so. Direction: include J3 in the lead.

9. **The same points are made several times.**
   - The WRITEY and BCONST kind lists appear in construction and again in the table's "After", nearly word for word.
   - "Kinds 0 and 9 to F have no line, so every signal is 0" (generalisation) is repeated as "A word of kind 0 or 9 to F gets none" (reflection) and again in the challenge task.
   - The one-edge argument ("Every instruction takes one edge, so its signals depend only on K and J" in motivation, "Gates suffice because … one edge" in generalisation) is made twice.

   Direction: keep each point once, where it is first needed.

10. **Investigation: "each other signal is an OR of the kind lines that need it"** is not true for AZERO, BYTE or OP2, which are ANDs only. OP1's OR also takes JOB1, an AND output, not only kind lines. The next bullet partly corrects this. Direction: qualify the sentence so it does not first state something the next one undoes.

11. **C appears before it is explained.** The prediction lead says "Its inputs are K, J and C" without saying C is the constant or why the decoder needs it; that only comes in the next figure's lead. Direction: give the one-clause reason where C first appears, or leave it out of the prediction lead.

12. **Fault lab: the cause comes from outside the drawing.** The figure is scoped to "decoder/signals", but the outcome reports "The cause shows `21`", which is worked out in the checks block, not drawn. LOAD's line is also made in "kind lines", not in the drawn block, yet the lead says "Each fault breaks one part of it". I could not confirm whether the fault lab lists CAUSED when scoped to `decoder/signals`. Direction: check what the learner sees, and say where the cause comes from.

13. **Small points.**
   - Challenge writes-text: "Allowed operators: `assign`, …". `assign` is not an operator.
   - signals-text's starting text has `default: WRITEY = 1'b0;`, which nothing explains. It is harmless after the zeroing, but a learner may wonder whether they must edit it.
   - Generalisation: "Lesson 3 adds them" is unclear right after a sentence about Module 8. Name it as this module's third lesson.
   - "one line goes high for each kind" reads as several lines at once. The meaning is one line per kind, high only for that kind.
   - Model versus reality: "adds to each instruction's path from the fetch to the register write". Stores and branches write no register.
