question: In this lesson you add kind 9 to your copy of the machine, end to end. Which parts of the machine would have to change?

motivation: This instruction does two things at its last edge:
- RY ← PC + 4, like a call: register Y keeps the address of the next instruction;
- PC ← RA + c, like a jump: the PC takes the address from a register.

A call goes to PC + 4c, a place fixed when the program is written. This instruction goes to the address in RA. The target comes from a register: whatever address the program puts in RA picks the place called.

It takes kind 9, job 0. Kind 9 is the first kind the machine leaves free.

For `R15 ← PC + 4, PC ← R4 + 0`, the word is `9040F000`: kind 9, job 0, A is R4, B is R0, Y is R15, constant 0.

construction: The datapath needs no new parts. The block "word for Y" already gives PC + 4 when CALL is 1. The next PC is already the ALU's result when JUMP is 1. The ALU already adds RA and the constant for a jump.

The controller needs no change. The new instruction sets CALL, so the controller's call sequence applies unchanged.

The decoder is the only place that changes. CALL and JUMP were kind lines (9.1: kind 6's line was CALL, kind 7's JUMP). With kind 9 both are ORs: CALL is an OR gate, orCall, of kind 6's line and kind 9's line, and JUMP an OR gate, orJump, of kind 7's line and kind 9's line; the lines of kinds 6 and 7 become KIND6 and KIND7.

- Add a column of signals for kind 9 that combines the call's and jump's signals.
- Kind 9, job 0 is now an instruction.
- Kind 9's other jobs are illegal.

explanation: In the drawn decoder, kind 9 has a line, KIND9. The construction shows how the OR gates orCall and orJump work out CALL and JUMP from the kind lines. KIND9 also joins the ORs of WRITEY, BCONST and OP1.

When CALL and JUMP are both 1, register Y takes PC + 4. The PC takes the ALU's result. In the machine's text, `if (JUMP) NEXT = RESULT;` comes after the line for CALL, so JUMP wins. The controller reads CALL at READ and goes to WRITE, as for a call.

generalisation: A new instruction can need four things: a column in the decoder, a place in the checks, its states, and new parts in the datapath. This one needed only the first two. Its transfers were ones the machine already made, and its edges were the call's.

An instruction the ALU cannot do needs more. Module 7's ALU does no shifts. A shift instruction would need a new part in the datapath.

In your copy, kinds A to F stay free. In the course's machine, kinds 9 to F do. Module 10 asks how to choose what to put there.
