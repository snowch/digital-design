question: Lesson 1 ended with a question: what should the machine do with a word that is no instruction?

Kinds 0 and 9 to F have no kind line. A register job has jobs 0 to 7, so job 8 is not a job.

The word `82000005` is kind 8, job 2, constant 5. That job copies a control register into register Y, and the constant says which one. A control register is one of five registers, numbered 0 to 4, that Module 12 builds. Module 8's machine stops at it, as a system job a later module builds.

The machine has no control register 5.

How does the decoder tell that a word is no instruction?

motivation: A word the machine must refuse is an **illegal instruction**: a word whose kind and job the decoder knows, or whose constant it refuses.

A word is illegal for one of three reasons:

- its kind is 0 or 9 to F;
- its job is one its kind does not define (jobs 8 to F of kinds 1, 2 and 5; jobs outside 0, 1, 8 and 9 of loads and stores; any job but 0 of calls and jumps; jobs 5 to F of kind 8);
- it is job 2 or 3 of kind 8, and its constant, read signed, names a control register outside 0 to 4.

At an illegal instruction, the decoder gives CAUSED `21`, and the machine stops.

Kind 8: job 0 gives `41`; jobs 1 to 3 stop it as jobs a later module builds; job 4 is `stop`.

Module 8's decoder read K and J only. The third reason needs the constant, so Module 9's decoder reads K, J and C.

explanation: The ILLEGAL signal is the OR of three checks on the K, J and C fields. One checks K: if the kind has no kind line, the check is 1. One checks J: if the kind does not define the job, the check is 1. One checks C: for certain kinds and jobs, the constant must be in range; otherwise, the check is 1. These checks are gates, like the control signals, and settle before the next edge. When ILLEGAL is 1, the cause block outputs `21`. The stop logic halts the machine. An illegal instruction changes no register and no memory word.

generalisation: A decoder that knows its kinds can refuse any word that does not match. Each kind has a kind line; a word with no line is illegal. The job check also refuses jobs the kind does not define. To add a new kind, a designer adds its kind line to the kind check and adds a term to the job check for its jobs. Nothing learns by itself. A jump to a wrong address runs whatever word is there. The three checks catch it, halt the machine, and report cause `21`, instead of running on.

construction: C is a 12-bit signed number. Values 0 to 4 are valid: bits 11 to 3 are all 0, and bits 2 to 0 are `000` to `100`. 

C is outside this range if any of bits 11 to 3 is 1. This catches values 8 to `7FF` (8 to 2047) and `800` to `FFF` (the negative numbers).

C is also outside the range if bit 2 is 1 with bit 1 or bit 0. This gives 5, 6 and 7.

You check both conditions.

The starting code checks only bits 2 to 0:

assign OUTSIDE = C[2] & (C[1] | C[0]);

The block has six parts:

- "kind check" outputs NOKIND, 1 when no kind line matches.
- "job check" outputs BADJOB, 1 when the job does not match its kind.
- "number check" outputs BADNUMBER, 1 when job 2 or 3 of kind 8 uses a number outside 0 to 4.
- An OR gate joins these three into ILLEGAL.
- SYSTEM outputs 1 for job 0 of kind 8; STOP outputs 1 for jobs 1 to 4.
- "cause" outputs CAUSED: `21` when ILLEGAL is 1, `41` for kind 8 job 0, `00` otherwise.
