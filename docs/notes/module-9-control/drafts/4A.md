question: In Module 8, one register transfer takes one edge. Here, R3 ← R1 - R2 takes 4 edges. What does each of those edges do? Which control signals make each edge work?

motivation: Each edge of an instruction performs one or two register transfers. We call one such transfer a **micro-operation**: an operation that happens at a single clock edge.

R3 ← R1 - R2 in Module 8 becomes four micro-operations:
- FETCH: IR ← memory[PC]
- READ: HA ← R1, HB ← R2
- ALU: HR ← HA - HB
- WRITE: R3 ← HR, and PC ← PC + 4

A register writes a word only if its enable signal is 1. IR has IREN; HA and HB share HOLDAB; HR has HOLDR; HM has HOLDM; register Y has WREG; the PC has PCEN.

The controller sets these enable signals from its state. Lesson 3 built the logic that decides which state comes next; this lesson builds the logic that sets the control signals for each state.

p1Question: Two edges of R1 ← -184 have passed. Which register takes a word at the next edge?

p1Explain: HR takes the word at the next edge: HR ← c, the constant -184. This edge is the ALU state, the instruction's third. The controller sets HOLDR to 1 there, and nowhere else.

IR took the instruction at the first edge, in FETCH. R1 is written at the fourth edge, in WRITE, taking its value from HR. At the same edge the PC moves to `004`.
