## investigation
The figure offers two lines: the gap rule, `display <= sensorA - sensorB`, and the CLASH rule, `if sensorA - sensorB >= 100 then lamps <= 4`. Choose one and watch the compiler take it one piece at a time. For each piece, the figure shows the piece, the rule that applies to it, the instruction written, and the line as it now reads.

## stepsLead
Choose the gap rule. Press "Next instruction" until nothing of the line is left, and read the rule each time. Then press an instruction in the table to see which piece of the line it came from. Do the same with the CLASH rule. Try both readings at the end: the shop's, and room A at -100.

## stepsOutcomes
The gap line became 4 instructions and `stop`. With the shop's readings, the display shows 66. The CLASH line became 7 instructions and `stop`. The branch's word is `56340003`. At a gap of 66, CLASH stays dark. With room A at -100, the gap is 150, and CLASH lights. Every instruction comes from one piece of the line.

## construction
The compiled CLASH line now runs on the whole machine from Module 13, with the shop's readings. The figure stops before the branch's FETCH edge, edge 19. The IR takes a word at that edge, and the figure asks for that word. As in 13.2, work it out from the fields. Work it out from the line `if R3 < R4 signed goto after1`. Kind 5 is a branch. Job 6 is "less than, read signed". A is R3, B is R4, and Y is unused. The constant counts the instructions the branch goes on, to `stop`.

## branchLead
Commit to the word first. Then press "Next edge" until the figure reaches edge 21, the branch's ALU edge. The condition block is open there. Watch MET and the PC as it does.

## branchQuestion
What word does the IR hold after the next edge?

## branchExplain
The IR takes `56340003`. K is 5, a branch. J is 6, less than, read signed. A is 3, for R3. B is 4, for R4. Y is 0, unused. The constant `003` is three instructions on: past `R5 <= 4` and the store, to `stop`.
The wrong options each break one field. `57340003` has job 7, "not less": the comparison is not turned over. `56430003` swaps R3 and R4. `56340007` counts the wrong distance.

## branchAfter
Before edge 21, MET was already 1. The ALU subtracts R4 from R3: 66 minus 100. The result is negative, so MINUS XOR OVER is 1. At edge 21, HR takes -34, and the PC takes `01C`, the address of `stop`. So CLASH stays dark. The line's `>=` is a comparison the machine has no instruction for. It ends at the condition block's gates, in MINUS XOR OVER, which Module 7 built.

## failureExperiment
The figure is the same compiler with one rule broken. Every piece now goes into R1, whatever is still waiting there. Before you press "Next instruction" to the end, predict what the display will show for the gap line. Then press through and check your prediction against the figure.

## faultLead
Press "Next instruction" until nothing of the line is left, then try both readings.

## faultOutcomes
The instructions are `R1 <= word[sensorA]`, `R1 <= word[sensorB]`, `R1 <= R1 - R1`, and `word[display] <= R1`. The second load writes over room A's reading before the subtraction reads it. `R1 - R1` is 0, so the display shows 0. The display shows 0 with the shop's readings, and also with room A at -30 and room B at 80. A register holds a piece until the job that uses it has read it. So the compiler must know which registers still hold pieces that wait.
