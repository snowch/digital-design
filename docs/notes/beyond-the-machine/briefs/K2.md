The section before this brief (the prediction) asked which branch the compiler writes for the
CLASH line; the reader has seen `if R3 < R4 signed goto after1`.

## The figure "the compiler at work"

Used in the investigation and the failure experiment. It shows, from the top: "The line as you
wrote it"; "The line now", with the next piece the compiler takes marked; the rule that applies to
that piece, in one sentence; three buttons, "Next instruction", "Back" and "Start again"; a count
of the instructions written; and a table of the instructions written so far. Pressing an
instruction in the table marks the piece of the line as written that it came from, and every
instruction that piece became. After the last piece, the table gains each instruction's address
and word, the program ends with `stop`, and the figure runs the program on the shop's readings and
says what the display and the lamps show. A choice of readings sits under it.

## investigation (section prose, 50 to 80 words)

The figure offers two lines to choose from: the gap rule, `display <= sensorA - sensorB`, and the
CLASH rule, `if sensorA - sensorB >= 100 then lamps <= 4`. The reader watches the compiler take one
piece at a time: the piece, the rule, the instruction written, the line rewritten.

## stepsLead (above the figure, 40 to 70 words)

Instructions: choose the gap rule, press "Next instruction" until nothing of the line is left, and
read the rule each time. Then press an instruction in the table to see which piece of the line it
came from. Then do the same with the CLASH rule, and try both readings at the end: the shop's, and
room A at -100.

## stepsOutcomes (shown after the last instruction, 60 to 90 words)

Facts: the gap line became 4 instructions and `stop`; the display shows 66 with the shop's
readings. The CLASH line became 7 instructions and `stop`; at a gap of 66 CLASH stays dark, and with
room A at -100 (gap 150) it lights. The branch's word is `56340003`. Every instruction comes from
one piece of the line.

## construction (section prose, 70 to 100 words)

Facts: the compiled CLASH line now runs on the whole machine from Module 13, with the shop's
readings. The figure is paused before the branch's FETCH edge, edge 19. It asks for the word the IR
takes at that edge, as 13.2's construction had a store's word worked out. Work it out from the line
`if R3 < R4 signed goto after1` and its fields: kind 5, a branch; job 6, less than, read signed;
A is R3; B is R4; Y unused; the constant counts the instructions it goes on, to `stop`. Do not give
the word or the constant's value.

## branchLead (above the figure, 40 to 70 words)

Instructions: commit to the word first. Then press "Next edge" to edge 21, the branch's ALU edge,
with the condition block open, and watch MET and the PC.

## branchQuestion (one sentence)

Asks what word the IR holds after the next edge.

## branchExplain (shown after commit, 50 to 80 words)

Facts: the IR takes `56340003`: K 5 a branch, J 6 less than read signed, A 3 for R3, B 4 for R4,
Y 0 unused, and the constant `003`, three instructions on, past `R5 <= 4` and the store to `stop`.
The wrong options, one short clause each: `57340003` is job 7, "not less", the comparison not
turned over; `56430003` swaps R3 and R4; `56340007` counts the wrong distance.

## branchAfter (shown once edge 21 has run, 60 to 90 words)

Facts: before edge 21, MET was already 1: the ALU subtracts R4 from R3, 66 minus 100, and the
result is negative, so MINUS XOR OVER is 1. At edge 21 HR takes -34 and the PC takes `01C`, the
address of `stop`, so CLASH stays dark. The line's `>=`, a comparison the machine has no instruction
for, ends at the condition block's gates, MINUS XOR OVER, which Module 7 built.

## failureExperiment (section prose, 40 to 70 words)

The figure is the same compiler with one rule broken: every piece goes into R1, whatever is still
waiting there. Ask the reader to predict what the display will show for the gap line, before they
press "Next instruction" to the end. Do not give the answer.

## faultLead (above the figure, 20 to 40 words)

Instructions: press "Next instruction" until nothing of the line is left, then try both readings.

## faultOutcomes (shown after the last instruction, 60 to 90 words)

Facts: the instructions are `R1 <= word[sensorA]`, `R1 <= word[sensorB]`, `R1 <= R1 - R1`,
`word[display] <= R1`. The second load writes over room A's reading before the subtraction reads
it, and `R1 - R1` is 0, so the display shows 0 for the shop's readings and for room A at -30 and
room B at 80. A register holds a piece until the job that uses it has read it, so the compiler must
know which registers still hold pieces that wait.
