# Brief 1A: lesson `instructions` (Module 8, lesson 1), first half

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here. This lesson introduces two
terms: **instruction** and **datapath**. It is the first lesson that may use either.

Keys to write: `question`, `motivation`, `prediction`, `p1Question`, `p1Explain`, `jobsLead`,
`jobsAfter`.

## question (the opening; about 100 words)

1. Module 7 ended asking where the ALU's two input words, A and B, come from, and where its Y and
   four flags go.
2. Module 6's register file gives two of its words at once, QA and QB, chosen by the read
   addresses RA and RB. At a rising edge of CLK it writes the word on D into the register WA
   names, when WE is 1.
3. Join them: QA to the ALU's A, QB to its B, and the ALU's Y back to the register file's D. Then
   one edge can make a register take a word the ALU worked out from two registers, such as
   `R3 ← R1 - R2`.
4. For that, the circuit needs four numbers at each edge: the ALU's three-bit code, and three
   register numbers, 4 bits each, since there are 16 registers.
5. End on the question: how does one word carry all four numbers to the parts that need them?

## motivation (about 80 words)

1. The shop's office keeps room A's reading in R1 and room B's in R2: -184 and -250, in tenths of a
   degree.
2. Module 7's flags lesson subtracted them: -184 - (-250) = 66.
3. The office will want many such jobs, one per edge, each on different registers.
4. The course's machine says what one edge does with an **instruction**: a 32-bit word whose
   digits name the job and the registers. Introduce the term here, plain meaning first.
5. Do not give the digit layout here; the prediction's figure shows it.

## prediction (the text above the figure; about 80 words)

1. The figure joins the register file and the ALU. A bus called IR carries the instruction
   `13123000`, which is `R3 ← R1 - R2`: kind 1 (a register job), job 3 (subtract), A is R1, B is
   R2, Y is R3, and a constant of 0 that this lesson does not use.
2. R1 holds -184 and R2 holds -250. Every other register is X: nothing has written it.
3. WRITEY is the register file's write enable, set to 1 by hand here: 1 means the next rising
   edge writes the ALU's result into the register the Y digit names.
4. Choose an answer and press "Check my prediction". The "Clock edge" button appears after.

## p1Question (one or two sentences)

`13123000` is on IR and WRITEY is 1. R1 holds -184 and R2 holds -250. After one rising edge of CLK,
what does R3 hold?

## p1Explain (shown after the learner commits; about 70 words)

1. R3 holds 66.
2. Kind 1 is a register job; job 3 is subtract; A is R1, B is R2 and Y is R3. So the edge does
   `R3 ← R1 - R2`, and -184 - (-250) = 66.
3. The ALU worked out 66 on its Y, the bus RESULT, before the edge. The edge wrote it into R3,
   which was X until then.
4. -434 is R1 + R2, job 2. -66 is R2 - R1, with A and B the other way round.

## jobsLead (the text above the investigation's figure; about 150 words, a short list allowed)

1. The drawing: IR comes in on the left. The block "digits" splits it into its six fields, K, J,
   A, B, Y and C. It holds no gates: each output is some of IR's wires.
2. A, B and Y go to the register file's RA, RB and WA. J goes to a block marked "split", named
   jobBits, which gives J's four bits one by one. J's bits 2 to 0 go to the ALU's OP2, OP1 and
   OP0. K, J's bit 3 and C go nowhere yet.
3. QA and QB go to the ALU's A and B. The ALU's Y is the bus RESULT. It goes back to the register
   file's D, and out at the right.
4. The register file, the ALU and the buses between them are the machine's **datapath**: the parts
   that hold words and work on them. Introduce the term here, plain meaning first.
5. Controls: choose an instruction from the list; press "Clock edge" for one rising edge; press
   WRITEY's pin to change it; press "Start again" to put every register back as it started.
   Press any wire to see its name and value.
6. 64-bit buses are too wide to write on the drawing. Their values are in the table "Buses". The
   table "Registers" shows R1 to R4, each as a word in hexadecimal and read signed, and marks
   "Written" beside a register the last edge wrote.
7. The list: `13123000` (R3 ← R1 - R2); `12123000` (R3 ← R1 + R2); `15024000` (R4 ← R2, job 5,
   copy B); `16101000` (R1 ← R1 + 1, job 6, count up); and `13123000` again with WRITEY at 0.

## jobsAfter (below the figure; about 100 words)

Each result below is from the start, R1 -184 and R2 -250:

1. R3 ← R1 - R2 writes 66 into R3. R3 ← R1 + R2 writes -434. R4 ← R2 writes -250 into R4.
   R1 ← R1 + 1 writes -183 into R1.
2. With WRITEY at 0, RESULT still shows 66, but the edge writes no register.
3. QA, QB and RESULT change as soon as an instruction arrives, with no edge. The edge only writes.
4. Pressing "Clock edge" again repeats the instruction: R1 ← R1 + 1 goes on to -182, then -181.
