# Brief 4C: lesson `memory-access` (Module 8, lesson 4), the end

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here, with one change: PC,
"program counter" and "fetch" are allowed now. This lesson introduces no term.

Keys to write: `writeMemoryLead`, `c2Task`, `c2Hints.1` to `c2Hints.5`, `reflection`,

## writeMemoryLead (one sentence)

Complete the datapath's text with its two new selectors.

## c2Task (about 190 words, a list allowed)

1. The text is the whole datapath of this lesson, a module called `machine`: PC, the course's
   `memory`, `decoder`, `registers`, `alu` and `stops` modules used inside it, the widening and
   pickB as in the last challenges.
2. Inputs: CLK, RST, SENSORA and SENSORB (64 bits each). Outputs: PC, HALT, CAUSE, DISPLAY and
   LAMPS.
3. Two selectors are missing. The starting text has `assign ALUA = QA;` and
   `assign YIN = RESULT;`. Replace them:
   - ALUA, the ALU's A input, is QA when AZERO is 0, and 0 when AZERO is 1.
   - YIN, the register file's D, is RESULT when LOAD is 0, and MQ when LOAD is 1.
4. The ROM holds this lesson's program. The tests set the sensors to -184 and -250, reset the
   machine, and run its 8 edges to the stop.
5. There are 10 tests. They check PC after each edge, the display from the fifth edge on, the
   lamps from the seventh, HALT and CAUSE at the stop, and that one more edge leaves PC at `01C`.
6. You may use what the earlier challenges used: `always_comb` and `case` among them.

## c2Hints (five rungs)

1. Each selector chooses between two words by one control signal, as pickB chooses by BCONST.
2. A common mistake: giving ALUA the constant when AZERO is 1. The constant goes to B; A takes 0.
3. ALUA: `case (AZERO)` with `1'b0: ALUA = QA;` and `1'b1: ALUA = 64'h0;`.
4. YIN: `case (LOAD)` with `1'b0: YIN = RESULT;` and `1'b1: YIN = MQ;`.
5. The whole answer, in place of the two assignments:

```
always_comb
  case (AZERO)
    1'b0: ALUA = QA;
    1'b1: ALUA = 64'h0;
  endcase

always_comb
  case (LOAD)
    1'b0: YIN = RESULT;
    1'b1: YIN = MQ;
  endcase
```

## reflection (about 100 words)

1. A load reads a word at A + c, or at c alone, and writes it into a register; a store writes a
   register's word there. The ALU's add gives the address.
2. The ROM, the RAM and the shop's devices are one memory, and the memory refuses an address it
   does not hold, a misaligned word, a byte at a device, and a store to anything read only.
3. Every program so far runs its instructions in order, from `000` to its `stop`.
4. End on the question: the office wants the lower of the two readings on the display. How can a
   program do one thing or another, depending on what it finds?

## modelVsReality (about 110 words)

1. In this model the memory answers a load within the edge, so a load takes one edge like any
   other instruction. A real memory is slower than the ALU, and a real machine often waits for it.
2. The model reads the instruction and the data from one memory at once. A real chip often keeps
   them apart, or adds small fast copies of memory close to the datapath.
3. The sensors here are words the figure sets. A real sensor's reading arrives on its own time,
   and a real design takes it into a register at an edge (Module 6).
