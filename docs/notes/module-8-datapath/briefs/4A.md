# Brief 4A: lesson `memory-access` (Module 8, lesson 4), first half

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here, with one change: PC,
"program counter" and "fetch" are allowed now (an earlier lesson introduced them). Use
**instruction**, **datapath**, program counter and fetch plainly. This lesson introduces no term.
The machine so far (lesson 3): PC names the next instruction; the ROM gives it on the bus IR; a
closed block, the "decoder", works out the control signals from K and J; the "stop logic" stops
the machine at `stop` or at anything it cannot run, with a cause such as 21.

Keys to write: `question`, `motivation`, `prediction`, `p1Question`, `p1Explain`,
`showMarginLead`, `showMarginAfter`.

## question (about 80 words)

1. Every instruction so far works on registers and constants alone.
2. Module 6 put the shop's devices at addresses: the sensors are words read at `7D8` (room A) and
   `7E0` (room B); the display shows the word written at `7C0`; the lamps take the word written at
   `7C8`. The RAM, from `400`, keeps words written there.
3. End on the question: how does an instruction read a word at an address, or write one?

## motivation (about 100 words)

1. Kind 3 is a load: `RY ← memory[RA + c]`, or `RY ← memory[c]`. Kind 4 is a store:
   `memory[RA + c] ← RB`, or `memory[c] ← RB`.
2. The job digit says the size and the address: job 0 is a word (8 bytes, at a multiple of 8) at
   RA + c; job 1 a byte at RA + c; job 8 a word at c alone; job 9 a byte at c alone.
3. c alone is an absolute address: every address the memory holds fits in the constant, so one
   instruction reaches any of them.
4. `380027D8` reads: kind 3 (load), job 8 (a word at c alone), Y is R2, constant `7D8`. It is
   R2 ← memory[`7D8`]: room A's reading.

## prediction (above the figure; about 110 words)

1. The figure adds the memory to the datapath: one block, "memory", holding the ROM, the RAM and
   the shop's devices at their addresses. It reads the instruction at PC, as before, and also a
   word at the address on its ADDR input.
2. ADDR is RESULT: the ALU's add works out the address.
3. A block "A or 0" (named pickA) gives the ALU's A input either QA or 0. The decoder's output
   AZERO chooses 0 for an absolute address.
4. The memory's output MQ is the word it reads. A word selector (named pickLoad) gives the
   register file's D either RESULT or MQ; the decoder's output LOAD chooses MQ.
5. The sensors read -184 (room A) and -250 (room B).
6. PC is `000`, where `380027D8` waits. Choose an answer and press "Check my prediction". The
   "Clock edge" button appears after.

## p1Question (one or two sentences)

`380027D8` has A digit 0 and the constant `7D8`. After the next edge, what does R2 hold?

## p1Explain (about 90 words)

1. R2 holds -184, room A's reading.
2. Job 8 sets AZERO to 1, so the ALU adds 0 and the constant: RESULT is `7D8`, the address of room
   A's sensor.
3. The memory reads the word there, -184, on MQ. LOAD is 1, so pickLoad gives MQ to the register
   file, and the edge writes it into R2.
4. 2008 is `7D8` itself, the address: what R2 would hold if LOAD did not choose MQ.
5. X is what R0 + `7D8` would give: R0 has never been written, so its word is X.

## showMarginLead (above the figure; about 150 words, a list allowed)

1. The program reads both rooms, works out the margin between them, keeps it in the RAM, shows it
   on the display, and lights two lamps:
   - `000` `380027D8`: R2 ← memory[`7D8`] (room A)
   - `004` `380037E0`: R3 ← memory[`7E0`] (room B)
   - `008` `13234000`: R4 ← R2 - R3
   - `00C` `48040400`: memory[`400`] ← R4 (kind 4, a store, job 8)
   - `010` `480407C0`: memory[`7C0`] ← R4 (the display)
   - `014` `25005005`: R5 ← 5
   - `018` `480507C8`: memory[`7C8`] ← R5 (the lamps)
   - `01C` `84000000`: stop
2. A store writes QB, register B's word, to the memory's D input. The decoder's output STORE says
   the edge writes.
3. The tables show the program, R2 to R5, the buses RESULT, QB, MQ and YIN (pickLoad's output),
   the devices (the display read signed, and the lamps as three bits), and the RAM's word at `400`.

## showMarginAfter (below the figure; about 80 words)

1. The program stops after 8 edges, at `01C`.
2. R2 holds -184, R3 -250, R4 66 and R5 5. The display shows 66, the RAM's word at `400` is 66,
   and the lamps show `101`.
3. A store writes no register: the decoder sets WRITEY to 0 for it.
