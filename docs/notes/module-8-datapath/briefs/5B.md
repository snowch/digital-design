# Brief 5B: lesson `branches` (Module 8, lesson 5), the middle

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here, with two changes: PC,
"program counter" and "fetch" are allowed, and "branch" is allowed (an earlier section of this
lesson introduces it in bold; here it is plain). The machine so far: PC, the ROM, the closed
"decoder", the "stop logic" and loads and stores. A branch (kind 5) compares RA with RB through
the ALU's RA - RB, and its job digit chooses the condition: 0 always, 1 never, 2 equal, 3 differ,
4 less unsigned, 5 not less unsigned, 6 less signed, 7 not less signed. The new block "condition"
gives MET; the block "next PC" gives NEXT.

Keys to write: `construction`, `writeConditionLead`, `c1Task`, `c1Hints.1` to `c1Hints.5`,
`branchFaultsLead`, `branchFaultsAfter`, `explanation`, `oneInstructionLead`,
`oneInstructionAfter`.

## construction (about 80 words)

1. The condition block is the job digit choosing one of eight answers from the flags of A - B.
2. Each pair of jobs is one test and its opposite: equal is ZERO, differ is NOT ZERO; less
   unsigned is NOT COUT, not less unsigned is COUT; less signed is MINUS XOR OVER, not less signed
   is its NOT. Job 0 is 1 and job 1 is 0.
3. The challenge below writes it with a `case` on J.

## writeConditionLead (one sentence)

Write the condition block: the job digit and the four flags in, MET out.

## c1Task (about 120 words, a list allowed)

1. Write a module called `condition`. Inputs: J (4 bits), ZERO, MINUS, COUT and OVER. Output: MET.
2. MET for each job: 0 gives 1; 1 gives 0; 2 gives ZERO; 3 gives NOT ZERO; 4 gives NOT COUT; 5
   gives COUT; 6 gives MINUS XOR OVER; 7 gives NOT (MINUS XOR OVER). Any other job gives 0.
3. The starting text has the `case` with job 0 and a `default` of 0. Add jobs 1 to 7.
4. You may use `module`, ports, `logic`, multi-bit signals, `assign`, selects, the bitwise
   operators, `always_comb` and `case`.
5. There are 32 tests: each job 0 to 7, with the flags of A - B for four pairs: -184 and -250,
   -250 and -184, 5 and 5, and 1 and -1.

## c1Hints (five rungs)

1. One line of the `case` per job, each giving MET one flag or a gate of flags.
2. A common mistake: using COUT for "less, unsigned". A - B carries out when A is not less than
   B, so less is NOT COUT.
3. `4'h2: MET = ZERO;` is equal.
4. `4'h6: MET = MINUS ^ OVER;` is less, signed, and `4'h7: MET = ~(MINUS ^ OVER);` its opposite.
5. The whole `case`:

```
always_comb
  case (J)
    4'h0: MET = 1'b1;
    4'h1: MET = 1'b0;
    4'h2: MET = ZERO;
    4'h3: MET = ~ZERO;
    4'h4: MET = ~COUT;
    4'h5: MET = COUT;
    4'h6: MET = MINUS ^ OVER;
    4'h7: MET = ~(MINUS ^ OVER);
    default: MET = 1'b0;
  endcase
```

## branchFaultsLead (above the figure; about 80 words)

1. The loop that adds 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1, with two faults to choose.
2. "MET held at 1": the condition block's output is 1 for every branch.
3. "MET held at 0": it is 0 for every branch.
4. Choose a fault and press "Run until it stops". Say first what the display will show.

## branchFaultsAfter (below the figure; about 100 words, a list allowed)

1. "MET held at 1": the branch is taken every time, also when R1 equals R0. The loop never ends:
   R1 counts down past 0 into negative numbers, and "Run until it stops" gives up after 500
   edges.
2. "MET held at 0": the branch is never taken. The loop runs once: the display shows 5, R1 holds
   4, and the machine stops after 8 edges.
3. Both faults leave every other instruction right: only the choice of the next instruction
   breaks.

## explanation (about 120 words)

1. The block "next PC" chooses NEXT from three words: PC + 4; the target, PC + 4c, where c is the
   widened constant and 4c is it times 4; and RESULT, the ALU's word.
2. NEXT is the target when the instruction is a branch and MET is 1. It is PC + 4 otherwise.
3. The decoder's output BRANCH is 1 for a branch. The ALU subtracts for every branch, and the
   condition block reads its flags.
4. Like every other instruction, a branch does its work in one edge: the next PC is worked out
   before the edge, and the edge writes it into PC.

## oneInstructionLead (above the capstone's figure; about 140 words)

1. This figure runs the colder-room program to `00C`, where R2 ← R3 waits, with R2 at -184 and R3
   at -250.
2. Press "Clock edge" once. Below the drawing, "The last edge, step by step" then lets you step
   through every step of the settle after that rising edge, with the slider or "Back a step",
   "Next step" and "Last step".
3. At each step, the drawing and the tables show the values at that step, and the line under the
   slider names the buses that changed at it.
4. Step 0 is the moment the clock rises, before anything has changed.

## oneInstructionAfter (below the figure; about 120 words)

1. At step 1, R2 takes -250: the register file writes at the edge.
2. At step 2, PC becomes `010`. At step 4, IR carries `480207C0`, the next instruction: the store
   to the display.
3. Then the decoder, the register file, the ALU and the memory work out that instruction's
   signals and words. On the way, some signals pass through values they do not keep: HALT is 1
   for some steps, and settles at 0 at step 42. GO settles at 1 at step 43.
4. The last change is at step 79.
5. Nothing writes during these steps: the display does not change. The next edge writes it.
