# Brief 7F: the words of four new figures

Read `00-module.md` first. The author asked for four drawings in Module 10. Each is drawn from the
course's own circuits and model; the words below go on or beside them. Facts only; write the
sentences. No em dashes. Backticks round hexadecimal values and addresses. A label is a few
words; a lead is a short paragraph that says what the figure shows and what to press.

Return each key as `key: text`.

## Lesson 1: the two machines' parts (opening figure, in the motivation)

The drawing has two outlines, one labelled "Module 8's machine" and one "Module 9's machine". They
overlap. Where they overlap is a band of four parts both machines have: R0 to R15, the PC, the
memory and the shop's devices. These are the parts a program can see. Above it, inside Module 8's
outline only, is a band with Module 8's IR (a bus, the ROM's output at the PC). Below it, inside
Module 9's outline only, is a band with Module 9's IR, HA, HB, HR, HM and the controller's state.

- `partsShared`: the label of the overlapping band. It must say both that a program can see these
  parts and that both machines have them.
- `partsOwn`: the label of each machine's own band (the outline round it already names the
  machine). It must say these parts are that machine's alone.

The explanation (lesson 1) now points at the drawing. Write one clause or short sentence for each:

- `explainSeen`: follows "Those parts are R0 to R15, the PC, the memory and the devices." Say the
  opening drawing puts them where the two machines' outlines overlap.
- `explainOwn`: follows "The agreement says nothing about the edges inside an instruction, nor
  about a circuit's own parts." Say those are the parts the opening drawing shows inside one
  machine's outline only.

## Lesson 3: every constant as an address (motivation, after the widening figure)

The figure lists every 12-bit constant used as an address made from the constant alone (0 plus
the constant), widened by the widen block, and what answers there. Five blocks, top to bottom:

- `000` to `3FF` widen to `0000000000000000` to `00000000000003FF`: the ROM.
- `400` to `7BF` widen to `0000000000000400` to `00000000000007BF`: the RAM.
- `7C0` to `7F7` widen to `00000000000007C0` to `00000000000007F7`: the shop's devices.
- `7F8` to `7FF` widen to `00000000000007F8` to `00000000000007FF`: no part. A load there stops the
  machine with cause `31`.
- `800` to `FFF` (bit 11 is 1) widen to `FFFFFFFFFFFFF800` to `FFFFFFFFFFFFFFFF`, negative
  numbers, -2048 to -1. No part answers there either; a load stops the machine with cause `31`.

Blocks that reach a part are shaded; the two that reach none are dashed. Nothing to press.

- `constantsCaption`: the figure's title (a few words): each constant as an address.
- `constantsRun`: the line naming a run of constants, with slots `{first}` and `{last}` (three hex
  digits each), for example "constants {first} to {last}".
- `constantsWiden`: the line giving the addresses they widen to, slots `{first}` and `{last}`
  (sixteen hex digits each).
- `constantsStop`: the line on a block no part answers, slot `{cause}`: a load there stops the
  machine with that cause.
- `captionConstantMap`: the caption under the figure, one sentence.
- `constantMapLead`: two or three sentences above the figure: it lists every constant read as an
  address; `000` to `7FF` reach the ROM, the RAM and the devices, with `7F8` to `7FF` left over
  (no part); `800` to `FFF` widen to negative words, far past the memory's last address.

## Lesson 4: where a left-out instruction would join (explanation, before the list of costs)

The drawing is a part of Module 8's datapath, built from the datapath's own blocks, with no
values shown and nothing to press:

- `pickB`, a word selector that gives the ALU's B: register B's word QB, or the widened constant
  WIDE when BCONST is 1.
- the ALU, with QA on A and the job on OP2, OP1 and OP0.
- the `+ 4` block, which makes PC + 4 from the PC.
- the block that chooses register Y's word, YIN: the ALU's result; MQ, the memory's word, when
  LOAD is 1; PC + 4 when CALL is 1.

These four are marked with dashed outlines. A multiplier or a shifter would sit beside the ALU and
reach register Y through that block, as a new source with a new control signal. A comparison with
zero needs a 0 on the ALU's B, through `pickB`. A call through a register needs nothing new: the
block already gives register Y PC + 4. A wider constant would change the layouts and the widen
block, which this drawing leaves out.

- `joinPlacesLead`: two or three sentences above the drawing: what it shows, and that the dashed
  outlines mark where the left-out instructions would join. Do not repeat the list of costs below
  it; name the parts.
- `captionJoinPlaces`: the caption, one sentence.
- `joinMark`: the words a screen reader gives each dashed outline: a place a left-out instruction
  would join.

## Lesson 5: the condition and its two uses (explanation)

The drawing is part of the datapath. The condition block takes J and the flags ZERO, MINUS, COUT
and OVER and gives MET, 1 when the job's condition holds. MET goes two ways:

- To the branch: an AND gate, `andTake`, gives 1 when BRANCH and MET are both 1. The word selector
  `pickTake` then gives NEXT, the next PC: PC4 (PC + 4) when the AND gives 0, TARGET when it gives
  1.
- To set if: the block met-word (`widenMet`) widens MET to a 64-bit word, 63 zeros above MET. The
  word selector `pickSet` gives YIN, register Y's word: HR when SET is 0, the widened MET when SET
  is 1.

The condition block is marked with a dashed outline. The figure opens with PC4 at `10`, TARGET at
`40` and HR at `42`, hexadecimal. Job 0 is met always; job 1 never. The learner sets J's bits,
presses BRANCH, SET and the flags on the drawing, and reads NEXT and YIN in the table below it
(the drawing writes no 64-bit word).

- `conditionUsesLead`: three or four sentences above the drawing: what it shows, the two uses,
  the opening values, and what to press and where to read.
- `captionConditionUses`: the caption, one sentence.
- `conditionMark`: the words a screen reader gives the dashed outline: the condition block, which
  both uses share.
