# Brief 2A: lesson `encoding` (Module 10, lesson 2), first part

Read `00-module.md` (the shared fact sheet) first; it applies here. "Instruction set" and
"microarchitecture" are allowed (lesson 1). This lesson introduces **opcode**, but only in the
explanation, which another brief writes: "opcode" must not appear in these keys. "Immediate" must
not appear anywhere.

Keys to write: `question`, `motivation`, `oneLayoutLead`, `prediction`, `p1Question`, `p1Explain`.

## question (about 80 words)

1. Lesson 1 ended on a question: why is every instruction laid out in whole hexadecimal digits,
   and what does that cost?
2. Every instruction is 32 bits: eight hexadecimal digits, K J A B Y and three digits of constant.
   Each field sits in the same digits in every instruction.
3. The layout is a choice. The same 32 bits could be cut up another way.
4. End on the question: what does this layout give, and what does it give up?

## motivation (above the figure; about 150 words, lists allowed)

1. The layout gives three things.
   - A person reads an instruction off its digits. `13123000` is kind 1, job 3 (subtract), A is
     R1, B is R2, Y is R3: R3 ← R1 - R2.
   - The register file's two read addresses and its write address are digits A, B and Y, wired
     straight from the instruction with no selector in front (Module 8).
   - The decoder never moves a field. The job is always digit 6; the constant is always digits 2
     to 0 (Module 9).
2. It costs three things.
   - The constant has 12 bits: -2048 to 2047.
   - There are 16 kinds, and the course's machine uses 8 of them, 1 to 8.
   - Most kinds leave a digit unused. A constant job and a load leave B unused. A store and a
     branch leave Y unused. A call leaves A and B unused. A jump leaves B and Y. A register job
     leaves the constant unused.

## oneLayoutLead (above the figure; about 50 words)

1. The figure cuts one instruction into its fields. Choose one of four: R3 ← R1 - R2; R2 ← R1 +
   100; R2 ← memory[`7D8`]; the colder-room program's branch, `56230002`.
2. Each field sits in the same digits whichever you choose.

## prediction (above the figure; about 90 words)

1. A packed layout gives a kind's unused register digits to its constant.
2. Take the constant job R2 ← R1 + 100. In the course's layout its word is `22102064`: K J A B Y,
   then three digits of constant. It leaves digit B unused.
3. In the packed layout, the constant takes four digits, digits 3 to 0, so it holds -32768 to
   32767. The constant's four digits sit side by side, and the other fields keep their order.
4. Choose an answer and press "Check my prediction". The figure then shows both layouts.

## p1Question (one sentence)

For R2 ← R1 + 100 in the packed layout, which field must move to another digit?

## p1Explain (about 70 words)

1. Y moves, from digit 3 to digit 4.
2. The constant's four digits must sit side by side, at digits 3 to 0. B's digit, 4, was the one
   left free, and Y takes it.
3. The word changes from `22102064` to `22120064`.
4. A stays in digit 5, and K and J stay where they were.
